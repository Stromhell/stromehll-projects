On the seventeenth of July 1936, the army in Spanish Morocco rebelled. The plan was led by General Emilio Mola. The army's best troops were there.

Franco had been sent away to the Canary Islands to keep him out of trouble. A British plane, the Dragon Rapide, secretly flew him to Morocco to take command.

The plotters expected the government to give up in days. It did not. In Madrid and Barcelona, the new prime minister Jose Giral handed out rifles to workers and unions.

The coup half worked. Spain split in two. The rebels, who called themselves Nationalists, held the west and north. The Republic held Madrid, Barcelona and most of the industry.

Franco's best troops were stuck in Africa, because the navy stayed loyal to the Republic. So German and Italian planes flew them across in one of the first big airlifts in history.

By October the other generals made Franco the head of state and commander. He called himself the Caudillo, the leader.
