On the twenty-third of February 1981, armed police burst into Spain's parliament during a live vote.

Their leader climbed onto the speaker's platform with a pistol and fired at the ceiling. Most of the deputies dived under their seats.

Spain had been a democracy for less than four years. For the thirty-six years before that, it had been ruled by one general. Now the army was trying to bring those days back.

That night, at about one in the morning, the king appeared on television in his army uniform. And what he said decided the fate of the country.

To understand that night, you have to go back to a war that killed hundreds of thousands of Spaniards, and the general who won it.
