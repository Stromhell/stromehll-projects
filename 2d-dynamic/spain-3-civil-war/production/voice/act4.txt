The war became a test for the bigger war to come. Hitler and Mussolini sent planes, tanks and troops to Franco. Stalin sent weapons and advisers to the Republic.

Britain and France stayed out. But around thirty-five thousand volunteers from more than fifty countries came to fight for the Republic in the International Brigades.

On the twenty-sixth of April 1937, a Monday, market day, German planes of the Condor Legion bombed the Basque town of Guernica for hours.

Much of the town was destroyed. Hundreds were killed. A few weeks later Pablo Picasso painted Guernica, and it became the most famous anti-war picture ever made.

The Republic was fighting itself as well. Communists, socialists, anarchists and others wanted different futures. In Barcelona in 1937 they fought each other in the streets.

The English writer George Orwell was there as a volunteer. A sniper shot him through the throat. He survived and wrote Homage to Catalonia.

Slowly, Franco's better-organised side won. Barcelona fell in January 1939, and Madrid at the end of March. On the first of April 1939, Franco declared the war over.

Estimates of the dead range from about three hundred and fifty thousand to half a million. Around half a million people fled to France.
