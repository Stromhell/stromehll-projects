On the twentieth of November 1975, Franco died. Two days later Juan Carlos became king.

He had sworn to keep Franco's system. Instead he picked a young Francoist official, Adolfo Suarez, as prime minister, and together they took it apart from the inside.

They persuaded Franco's own parliament to vote itself out of existence. In April 1977 they legalised the Communist Party, which shocked the army.

In June 1977 Spain held its first free election in forty-one years. In 1978 a new constitution made Spain a democracy with a king who reigns but does not rule, and gave Catalonia and the Basque Country their own governments.

Which brings us back to February 1981. While the gunmen held parliament, tanks rolled into the streets of Valencia.

The plotters thought the king would back them. Instead, at a quarter past one in the morning, he went on television and ordered the army to support the constitution.

By the next morning the coup was over. The gunmen walked out of the parliament and surrendered.

Spain joined NATO in 1982 and the European Community in 1986. A country that had spent forty years cut off became one of Europe's most visited places.

From Tariq's seven thousand men in 711, to the Alhambra in 1492, to an empire of silver, to a civil war and a dictator. Spain broke apart again and again. And each time, it put itself back together.
