The new government moved fast. A new constitution in 1931 separated church and state, gave women the vote, and promised land to poor farmers.

Manuel Azana, the prime minister, cut the army's officer corps, closed religious schools and gave Catalonia its own government.

To the left these reforms were too slow. To the right, they were an attack on everything Spain stood for. Churches were burned. Strikes and street fights became normal.

In 1933 the right won the election. In 1934 miners in Asturias rose up and declared a revolution. The government sent the army from Morocco. One of the generals directing it was Francisco Franco.

In February 1936 the left united as the Popular Front and won. Political killings grew on both sides.

On the thirteenth of July 1936, police officers took the monarchist leader Jose Calvo Sotelo from his home and shot him. Four days later, the army moved.
