Franco ruled alone. His party, the Falange, was the only legal one. Tens of thousands of Republicans were executed after the war, and many more were sent to prison camps.

Catalan, Basque and Galician were pushed out of public life. Everything had to be Spanish, Catholic and obedient.

Then the Second World War began. In October 1940 Hitler met Franco at Hendaye, on the French border, to bring Spain into the war.

Franco asked for too much: French colonies in Africa, weapons and food. Hitler later said he would rather have three or four teeth pulled than go through that meeting again.

Spain stayed officially out of the war. But Franco sent about forty-five thousand volunteers, the Blue Division, to fight for Germany against the Soviet Union.

After 1945 Spain was shut out by the winners. Franco tried to make Spain feed itself without trade. It failed. The 1940s were called the years of hunger.

Then the Cold War saved him. The Americans needed allies against communism. In 1953 they signed a deal for military bases in Spain in exchange for money. In 1955 Spain joined the United Nations.

From 1959 Spain opened up its economy. Factories, cars and millions of tourists followed. They called it the Spanish miracle.

In 1969 Franco named his successor: Prince Juan Carlos, grandson of the last king. He had been raised in Spain under Franco's eye. Everyone thought he would keep the system going.

In December 1973 the Basque group ETA killed Franco's prime minister, Admiral Carrero Blanco. A bomb under a Madrid street threw his car over the roof of a church building.
