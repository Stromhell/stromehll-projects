# From Eating Rats to Bullet Trains: How Japan Rebuilt Itself

Working title: **Fall Seven Times, Stand Up Eight: Japan's Comeback**

6 parts (cold open + 5 acts), 42 shots, 1058 words, about 7:14 at 160 wpm.

**Running device:** One family, the Sakais, is followed from 1945 to 1964: a mother and her two children in a burnt Tokyo shack, a father back from the war, a son who becomes a factory worker, a daughter who becomes an engineer. A small daruma doll on their shelf gets knocked over in every act and is set upright again. A GDP line paints itself along the bottom of the map in the background, from the floor to third in the world.

**Look:** 2D dynamic, locked Village Painterly look: 6-head characters from the village-cast rig (smooth tapered limbs, detailed 4-finger hands, nose below the eye line), in Japanese dress of the time (monpe trousers, school uniforms, work overalls, 1960s suits). Highly detailed painted backgrounds with warm light and dynamic cast shadows: burnt Tokyo under grey skies, black markets under rail bridges, terraced rice villages, small workshops, factory floors, the 1964 Shinkansen on a blue sky with Mount Fuji. Moving cameras, 3 to 5 parallax layers, characters acting in every shot, match-cut transitions. The bombings are shown through aftermath and light, never gore. SFX under the VO; no music until combine.
