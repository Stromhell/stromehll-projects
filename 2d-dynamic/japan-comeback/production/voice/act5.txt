On the first of October 1964, the world's first high-speed train, the Tokaido Shinkansen, left Tokyo for Osaka at up to two hundred and ten kilometres an hour.

Nine days later, Tokyo opened the first Olympic Games ever held in Asia.

The last runner carrying the Olympic flame was nineteen-year-old Yoshinori Sakai. He was born in Hiroshima on the sixth of August 1945, the day the bomb fell.

Hiroshima itself had been rebuilt. By the late 1950s its population was back to where it had been before the war.

In 1968 Japan passed West Germany. It was now the third-largest economy in the world, behind only the United States and the Soviet Union.

In 1980 Japan made more cars than America. By the late 1980s, Japanese firms made most of the world's memory chips. American business schools started writing books on how to learn from Japan.

There is a Japanese saying: nana korobi ya oki. Fall seven times, stand up eight.

Japan was knocked down harder than almost any country in modern history. It did not stand back up because of one plan. It stood up because millions of people kept getting up, one day at a time.
