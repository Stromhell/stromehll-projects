Then the world changed. China was going communist. The Soviet Union was next door. America suddenly needed Japan strong, not weak.

In 1949 an American banker, Joseph Dodge, cut subsidies and fixed the yen at three hundred and sixty to the dollar. Prices calmed down, but companies started laying people off. Japan was close to a depression.

Toyota was nearly bankrupt. In 1950 it cut over a thousand jobs, and its founder Kiichiro Toyoda resigned.

On the twenty-fifth of June 1950, North Korea invaded South Korea. American forces rushed in, and they needed trucks, uniforms and parts, fast. Japan was right next door.

The US Army ordered thousands of trucks from Toyota. The factory lights came back on.

Across Japan, war orders brought in billions of dollars. Factory output jumped. The governor of the Bank of Japan called it divine aid.

But it was an uncomfortable gift. A country rebuilt by one war was now growing rich from another.

In September 1951 Japan signed a peace treaty in San Francisco, and in April 1952 the occupation ended. India did not attend. It signed its own, more generous treaty with Japan in 1952.
