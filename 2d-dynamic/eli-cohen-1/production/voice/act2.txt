In Israel, he worked as a clerk and translator. In 1959 he married Nadia, and they started a family.

He had already been turned down once by Israel's intelligence services. But in 1960 the army's intelligence branch came back to him.

Israel wanted a man inside Syria. Syrian guns on the Golan Heights shelled Israeli farms below, and Israel knew little about what was up there.

For months, he trained: Syrian dialect, the Quran and Muslim prayer, codes, invisible ink, and the radio key.

They built him a new life: Kamel Amin Thaabet, a Syrian Muslim whose family had moved to Argentina, now a successful businessman who longed to go home.
