# Why New Year Is on January 1st

Working title: **The Wild History of New Year**

6 parts (cold open + 5 acts), 32 shots, 585 words, about 4:08 at 160 wpm.

**Running device:** The countdown. The video opens on a Times Square countdown that freezes at 'one' with a record scratch, rewinds 4,000 years, and every act ends with a little countdown clock ticking toward the present. The last shot finishes the countdown: ten to one, fireworks, HAPPY NEW YEAR. Janus, the two-faced god (one face looking back, one forward), is the mascot who turns up in each era.

**Look:** 2D dynamic, locked Village Painterly look, played for fun: 6-head characters with big reactions (yawns, double takes, shocked faces). Painted backgrounds with warm light and cast shadows: Babylon's ziggurat at spring sunrise with barley fields, the Nile in flood, a Chinese village with red lanterns, early Rome on its hills, Caesar's senate, the shrine of Janus, a snowy medieval village and church, the Vatican hall of 1582, a Georgian London street in 1752, snowy Moscow in 1918, Times Square at night with the ball and fireworks. Bouncy music, lots of comic SFX (record scratch, boing, cash-register ding for dates, party horns).

## Fact-check fixes against the transcript

- Transcript says Britain adopted the Gregorian calendar on 2 September 1751. The switch happened in 1752 (Calendar (New Style) Act 1750): Wednesday 2 September 1752 was followed by Thursday 14 September 1752. Script uses 1752. Britain moved New Year's Day to 1 January for 1752 (from 25 March).
- Rome: the earliest calendar (attributed to Romulus) had 10 months and 304 days, with the winter period unassigned; Numa Pompilius, the second king, added Ianuarius and Februarius. Script orders it that way (transcript had the order reversed).
- Julian calendar introduced 46 BCE, in force from 1 January 45 BCE.
- Gregorian reform, 1582: Thursday 4 October 1582 was followed by Friday 15 October 1582 (10 days dropped). Script says 'jumped forward ten days'.
- Russia adopted the Gregorian calendar in February 1918 (31 Jan 1918 followed by 14 Feb).
- Akitu: Babylonian spring festival around the vernal equinox (month of Nisannu), 11 days in the later Babylonian form; it honoured Marduk, whose victory over Tiamat is told in the Enuma Elish.
- Times Square ball drop: since 1907 (not stated in VO).
