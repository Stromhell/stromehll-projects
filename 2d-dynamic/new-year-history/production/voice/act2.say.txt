The Romans also started their year in March, around the spring equinox.

Rome's first calendar had just ten months and three hundred and four days. The middle of winter simply didn't belong to any month.

Back when Rome was still a kingdom, its second king added two new months: Yan-oo-ah-ree-us and Feb-roo-ah-ree-us.

But the calendar still didn't match the sun. Over time, festivals drifted into the wrong seasons.

Hundreds of years later, Julius Caesar fixed it with the Julian calendar, and made January the first the first day of the year.

From then on, Romans welcomed the year with offerings to Janus, the two-faced god of doorways, who looks back at the old year and forward to the new. January is named after him.

And here's the strange part: they always made sure to do at least a little work on New Year's Day. Yes, really.
