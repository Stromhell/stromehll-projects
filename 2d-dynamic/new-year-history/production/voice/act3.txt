Then came medieval Europe, and the New Year's party got cancelled.

Church leaders saw it as un-Christian, and swapped it for holidays like Christmas. Which, funnily enough, had pagan roots of its own.

For a while, January the first was just... an ordinary day.

Then, in 1582, Pope Gregory the Thirteenth stepped in. The calendar had drifted away from the sun again, so he created a new one: the Gregorian calendar.

To catch up, it jumped forward ten days. And New Year's Day was back on January the first.

Many Catholic countries in Europe adopted it quickly. Others took a lot longer.
