The first recorded New Year's celebrations happened around two thousand B C E, in ancient Babylon.

And it wasn't in winter. The Babylonians partied in late March, at the spring equinox, when the crops came back to life.

Their festival was called Ah-kee-too, and it lasted eleven days. Eleven! Most of us can barely make it to midnight.

Ah-kee-too also celebrated the sky god Mar-dook beating the sea goddess Tee-ah-mat. Basically a divine wrestling match, and the sky won.

Soon other cultures joined the party. The ancient Egyptians celebrated their new year around the yearly flooding of the Nile.

And in China, people began celebrating the New Year around three thousand years ago.
