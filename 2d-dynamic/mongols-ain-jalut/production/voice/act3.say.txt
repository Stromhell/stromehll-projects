Baghdad had been the capital of the Ab-bass-id caliphs for five hundred years: a city of scholars, markets and libraries, including the famous House of Wisdom.

But the caliphate's power was long gone. The caliph al-Moos-tah-sim had a weak army and believed the Muslim world would rise to defend him.

In January twelve fifty-eight, Hoo-lah-goo's army, with Chinese siege engineers, surrounded the city. On the tenth of February, Baghdad surrendered.

For about a week, the city was sacked. Estimates of the dead range from tens of thousands to many hundreds of thousands.

The libraries were looted. Later writers said the Tigris ran black with ink from the books thrown into it.

The caliph was killed, according to the accounts, rolled in a carpet and trampled by horses, because the Mongols did not spill royal blood. The Ab-bass-id caliphate in Baghdad was over.
