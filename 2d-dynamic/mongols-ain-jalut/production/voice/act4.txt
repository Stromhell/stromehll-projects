In early 1260, the Mongols took Aleppo, and Damascus surrendered. Only one great power was left in the region: Egypt.

Egypt was ruled by the Mamluks: soldiers who had been bought as boys, often from the Turkic steppe, trained for war, and freed.

Many had been sold because the Mongols had overrun their homelands. One of them was a Kipchak Turk with one blue-white eye, named Baybars.

Their new sultan, Qutuz, had been a slave too. He read Hulagu's letter. Then he had the Mongol envoys executed and their heads hung on Cairo's gate.

There was no going back now.

Then luck turned. Far away in China, the Great Khan Möngke had died. Hulagu took most of his army back east, leaving his general Kitbuqa with perhaps ten to twenty thousand men.
