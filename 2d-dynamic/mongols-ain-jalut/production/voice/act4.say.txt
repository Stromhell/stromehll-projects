In early twelve sixty, the Mongols took Aleppo, and Damascus surrendered. Only one great power was left in the region: Egypt.

Egypt was ruled by the Mam-looks: soldiers who had been bought as boys, often from the Turkic steppe, trained for war, and freed.

Many had been sold because the Mongols had overrun their homelands. One of them was a Kip-chak Turk with one blue-white eye, named Bye-bars.

Their new sultan, Koo-tooz, had been a slave too. He read Hoo-lah-goo's letter. Then he had the Mongol envoys executed and their heads hung on Cairo's gate.

There was no going back now.

Then luck turned. Far away in China, the Great Khan Mung-keh had died. Hoo-lah-goo took most of his army back east, leaving his general Kit-boo-ka with perhaps ten to twenty thousand men.
