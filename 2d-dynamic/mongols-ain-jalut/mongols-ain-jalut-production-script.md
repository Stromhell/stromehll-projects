# The Battle That Stopped the Mongols

Working title: **The Battle That Stopped the Mongols**

7 parts (cold open + 6 acts), 36 shots, 772 words, about 5:22 at 160 wpm.

**Running device:** The envoy's paiza: the golden Mongol tablet that every Mongol messenger wore as a passport. It hangs on the envoy killed at Otrar in 1218, which starts the war; it hangs on the envoys whom Sultan Qutuz executes in Cairo in 1260; and the last shot is a paiza lying in the grass at Ain Jalut. Envoys start and end the story. A map of Asia returns in every act: Mongol grey spreads west, then stops at a line in 1260.

**Look:** 2D dynamic, locked Village Painterly look: 6-head characters from the village-cast rig in 13th-century dress (Mongol deel coats, fur hats and lamellar armour; Khwarazmian silks and turbans; Abbasid court robes; Mamluk mail, helmets and yellow banners). Highly detailed painted backgrounds with warm light and dynamic cast shadows: steppe grass under huge skies, the walled caravan city of Otrar, blue-tiled Samarkand, Baghdad on the Tigris with the House of Wisdom, Cairo's Bab Zuweila gate, the green valley of Ain Jalut below Mount Gilboa. Moving cameras, 3 to 5 parallax layers, characters act in every shot. Massacres are shown through empty streets, smoke and the map, never gore. Neutral narration: no prophecy framing, no modern politics. SFX under the VO; music at combine.

## Fact-check fixes against the transcript

- Source frames the war as a fulfilled hadith prophecy (Gog and Magog) and adds a modern Palestine comparison and one-sided lines about Christians. All removed per Pushpam's OK for a neutral history video; the script follows standard histories (Juvayni, Rashid al-Din, Ibn al-Athir as summarised by modern historians).
- Otrar 1218: the caravan is usually given as about 450 merchants; governor Inalchuq (Ghayir Khan). Shah Muhammad II killed the lead envoy (some accounts: one beheaded, two had beards shaved).
- Shah Muhammad II died in 1220/21 on an island in the Caspian (Abaskun).
- Baghdad siege began late Jan 1258; city surrendered 10 Feb 1258; caliph al-Musta'sim killed ~20 Feb. Death toll estimates vary widely (90,000 to 800,000+ in later sources); script gives a range.
- The 'Tigris ran black with ink' is a later chroniclers' image; the script attributes it.
- Möngke died Aug 1259; news led Hulagu to withdraw most of his army in 1260; Kitbuqa (a Nestorian Christian) left with an estimated 10,000-20,000 troops.
- Qutuz executed Hulagu's envoys and displayed their heads at Bab Zuweila, Cairo (1260).
- Ain Jalut: 3 Sept 1260, Jezreel Valley, Galilee. Baybars led the vanguard; feigned retreat; Qutuz's 'Wa Islamah!' cry from Mamluk chronicles. Kitbuqa killed.
- Ain Jalut is often called the first major Mongol defeat; earlier Mongol setbacks existed (e.g. Parwan 1221, Volga Bulgaria 1223), so the script says 'the first time ... the defeat had stuck' toward the west.
- Qutuz was assassinated 24 Oct 1260 by emirs including Baybars; Baybars ruled 1260-1277.
- Berke (Golden Horde) converted to Islam and fought Hulagu from 1262; Ghazan of the Ilkhanate converted in 1295.

## Removed from the transcript

- Cut the prophecy framing, the Umayyad/Abbasid background lecture, and modern political comparisons. Kept only the history from Otrar (1218) to Ghazan (1295).
