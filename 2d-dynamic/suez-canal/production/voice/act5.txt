So the French builders turned to machines. Giant steam dredgers, excavators and long conveyor chutes arrived from Europe.

They burned coal day and night and moved more earth than whole armies of men. In the last years, most of the digging was done by machine.

In 1866, the workers dug something out of the sand: one of Darius's stones, buried for more than two thousand years.

In August 1869, the Mediterranean water finally flowed into the Bitter Lakes, and on to the Red Sea.

On the seventeenth of November 1869, the canal opened. France's Empress Eugenie led a parade of ships on her yacht, the Aigle. Kings and princes came from across Europe.

Egypt's ruler Ismail threw one of the most expensive parties in history: fireworks, banquets, thousands of guests. Cairo even got a new opera house.
