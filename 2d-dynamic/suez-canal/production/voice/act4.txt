Digging began in April 1859. At first, there were almost no machines. The work was done by Egyptian farmers, forced to come, around twenty thousand at a time.

They dug with picks, carried sand in baskets on their backs, and fresh water came by camel, or by boat along a new canal from the Nile.

In 1865 cholera swept through the camps. Thousands died. Nobody knows the total death toll of the whole project. Estimates run from several thousand to over a hundred thousand.

Meanwhile, Britain pushed the Ottoman sultan, Egypt's overlord, to ban the forced labour. In 1864 it was stopped.

Overnight the work camps emptied. Half-dug trenches, no workers, money running out. Many in Europe thought the canal was dead.
