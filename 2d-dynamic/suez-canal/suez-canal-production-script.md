# 3,000 Years, 6 Empires, 1 Ditch: The Suez Canal Story

Working title: **The Ditch Every Empire Wanted**

7 parts (cold open + 6 acts), 39 shots, 973 words, about 6:39 at 160 wpm.

**Running device:** Darius's granite stone. Every act passes the same patch of desert where Darius of Persia set up a carved stone around 500 BC. We see it raised, buried by sand, half-uncovered by Roman barges, buried again, and finally dug out by de Lesseps' workers in 1866, as it really was. A parchment map of the Mediterranean, Red Sea and the long route round Africa returns in every act.

**Look:** 2D dynamic, locked Village Painterly look: 6-head characters from the village-cast rig in the dress of each era (pharaonic linen, Persian robes, Roman tunics, Ottoman kaftans, French uniforms of 1798, 1860s frock coats, Egyptian galabiyas). Highly detailed painted desert backgrounds with warm light and dynamic cast shadows: Nile delta fields, a reed-basket trench under a white sun, Darius's stone, Roman wooden lock gates, an Ottoman palace map room, Napoleon's surveyors with brass instruments, a cholera camp at night, steam dredgers puffing black smoke, the 1869 opening with fireworks, a modern container ship. Moving cameras, 3 to 5 parallax layers, characters acting in every shot, match-cut transitions. Death is shown through empty tools and graves, never gore. SFX under the VO; no music until combine.

## Fact-check fixes against the transcript

- Source says Darius's canal was 'so narrow two warships could not pass'. Herodotus says the opposite: wide enough for two triremes to row side by side. Removed; the script says it silted up instead.
- Source credits Romans with the first locks. The lock (a gate keeping salt water out of the Nile) is credited to Ptolemy II, around 270 BC. Script fixed; Trajan's reopening kept.
- Necho II's attempt: c. 610-595 BC. The 120,000 dead figure and the oracle are from Herodotus (Histories 2.158); script attributes both to him.
- Darius I's Suez stelae (e.g. the Chalouf stela) are real; the quoted line follows the standard translation. The Chalouf stela was found in 1866 during the canal works (Charles de Lesseps).
- Added the Arab period: Amr ibn al-As reopened the canal c. 641; Abbasid caliph al-Mansur had it filled in c. 767 to cut supplies to rebels in Medina.
- Ottoman plan by Grand Vizier Sokollu Mehmed Pasha dated 1568; it was abandoned (wars, cost).
- Napoleon's engineer Jacques-Marie Le Père (1798-99) estimated the Red Sea about 30 feet (~10 m) higher; disproved by surveys in 1847 (Société d'Études, Bourdaloue and others).
- De Lesseps' concession from Said Pasha: 30 Nov 1854; work started 25 April 1859. Palmerston opposed it.
- Forced labour (corvée): about 20,000 workers at a time on rotation; source's 'hundreds of thousands at once' changed. Total deaths are disputed (from several thousand to an often-cited but contested 120,000); script gives the range.
- Cholera epidemic in 1865. Forced labour ended in 1864 after British pressure on the Ottoman sultan and Napoleon III's arbitration.
- Steam dredgers and elevators by Borel, Lavalley & Cie did most of the excavation in the final years.
- Mediterranean water reached the Bitter Lakes in August 1869; opening ceremony 17 Nov 1869 with Empress Eugénie on L'Aigle. Cairo's Khedivial Opera House opened in 1869 (Aida premiered there in 1871).
- 1875: Khedive Ismail sold Egypt's 44% stake; Disraeli bought it with a £4 million loan from N M Rothschild & Sons. British occupation from 1882.
- 1956: Nasser nationalised the canal on 26 July; Suez Crisis; British, French and Israeli forces withdrew under US and Soviet pressure.
- Ever Given blocked the canal 23-29 March 2021; Lloyd's List estimated about $9.6 billion of trade held up per day. Script says 'around ten billion'.
- About 12% of global trade passes through Suez (pre-2024 figure); route round the Cape of Good Hope adds roughly 7,000-9,000 km depending on the trip. Script says 'around eight thousand'.

## Removed from the transcript

- Source's 'fortune-teller panicked the pharaoh' kept as Herodotus' oracle. Dropped the comment-bait closing question. Added the Arab period and the Darius stone rediscovery for a stronger through-line.
