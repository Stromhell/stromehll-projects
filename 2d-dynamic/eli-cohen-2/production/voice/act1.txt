After the 1963 coup, Eli's friends were running the country. Officers took him up to the Golan Heights, the hills above Israel's northern farms.

He saw bunkers, gun positions and trenches. He remembered everything, and sent it home.

The most famous story: he told the officers their soldiers were baking in the sun, and suggested planting eucalyptus trees beside each position for shade.

The story goes that in 1967, Israeli soldiers used those trees to find the bunkers. Historians still debate how much is true, but the legend stuck.

He also reported on Syria's plan to divert the headwaters of the Jordan River, which would have cut Israel's water supply. The work sites came under Israeli attack in the years that followed.
