Before dawn on the eighteenth of May 1965, he was hanged in Marjeh Square in central Damascus. His body was left in public for hours, and the event was broadcast.

Syria never returned his body. His family says the grave was moved several times so it could never be found.

Two years later, in the 1967 war, Israel captured the Golan Heights. How much his reports helped is still debated, but in Israel he became a national hero.

In 2018, Israel's intelligence service recovered his wristwatch. In 2025, it said it had brought back thousands of documents and belongings from his Syrian file.

Nadia Cohen spent her life asking for his remains. For one country he was a hero; for the other, a traitor who had fooled them all. Either way, nobody had fooled them better.
