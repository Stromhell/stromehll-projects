Description:
For about two centuries one red mountain in Bolivia produced a huge share of all the silver in the world. That silver paid for armies, palaces and wars on three continents and made Spain the empire on which the sun never set. Yet the king who owned it went bankrupt four times.

This is Part 2 of our Spain series. It starts with a sailor who got lost: Columbus, sure he had reached Asia. Then Cortes and the fall of the Aztecs, Pizarro and the room full of gold, the silver mines of Potosi and the coin that became the model for the dollar. Then Philip II, El Escorial, Lepanto and the Armada, a long decline, Napoleon, the wars of independence, and finally 1898, when one explosion in Havana harbour ended a 400-year empire.
