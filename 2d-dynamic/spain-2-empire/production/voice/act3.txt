In 1545 the Spanish found the Cerro Rico at Potosi, a mountain full of silver, almost four thousand metres up in the Andes.

They forced Indigenous villages to send men to work in the mines under a system called the mita. Many never came home.

The silver was melted, stamped into coins and shipped to Spain in huge treasure fleets, guarded by warships against pirates.

Another route went west. From 1565 the Manila galleons carried Mexican silver across the Pacific to the Philippines, to buy Chinese silk and porcelain.

The Spanish silver coin, the piece of eight, was used from China to Europe. It became one of the first truly global currencies. The American dollar was based on it.

But so much silver poured into Europe that prices kept rising for a century. Spain had the money, but it did not build much with it.
