What was left were islands: Cuba, Puerto Rico and the Philippines. Cuba was the jewel, rich from sugar, and its people wanted freedom.

In 1895 Cuba rose in revolt. The United States, ninety miles away, watched closely. American newspapers printed shocking stories every day to sell more copies.

On the fifteenth of February 1898, the American warship USS Maine blew up in Havana harbour. Two hundred and sixty-six men died. Nobody proved what caused it, but the US blamed Spain.

In May, an American squadron destroyed the Spanish fleet in Manila Bay in a single morning. In July, the Spanish fleet off Cuba tried to break out and was wiped out too.

In the Treaty of Paris in December 1898, Spain gave up Cuba, Puerto Rico, Guam and the Philippines. The US paid twenty million dollars for the Philippines.

In Spain they called it simply the Disaster. Four hundred years after Columbus, the empire that began with a lucky mistake was gone.

And without an empire, Spain turned on itself. Within forty years it would be in one of the bloodiest civil wars of the century.
