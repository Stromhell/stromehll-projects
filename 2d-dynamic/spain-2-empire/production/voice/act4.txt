In 1516 the grandson of Ferdinand and Isabella became king. Charles the First of Spain also inherited Austria, the Netherlands and parts of Italy, and was elected Holy Roman Emperor as Charles the Fifth.

His lands circled the globe. When the sun set on Spain, it was rising on Spanish lands in the Americas or Asia.

His son Philip the Second made Madrid the capital in 1561 and built El Escorial, a palace and monastery so big it has thousands of doors and windows.

He fought everyone. In 1571 his fleet, with Venice and the Pope, beat the Ottomans at Lepanto. In 1580 he took Portugal and its empire too.

But Protestant rebels in the Netherlands fought him for eighty years. And Protestant England kept attacking his treasure ships.

In 1588 Philip sent the Armada, about one hundred and thirty ships, to invade England.

English fire ships broke the formation, and storms did the rest. The fleet was blown around the north of Scotland and Ireland. Perhaps half the ships came home.

Philip stopped paying his debts four times: in 1557, 1560, 1575 and 1596. The silver came in, and it went straight out again to pay for wars.

This was also Spain's Golden Age of art. Cervantes wrote Don Quixote. Velazquez painted Las Meninas. The empire was rich in culture even as it went broke.
