# How Spain Ruled the World, Then Lost Everything

Working title: **The Empire Built on a Mistake: Spain, 1492 to 1898**

7 parts (cold open + 6 acts), 54 shots, 1332 words, about 9:06 at 160 wpm.

**Running device:** A painted silver coin, the Spanish dollar (piece of eight), spins in the corner of every act. It shines brighter as the empire grows, is chipped by each bankruptcy, and at the end of 1898 falls flat and stops spinning. The parchment map from part 1 returns, now spreading Spanish gold across the Americas and the Pacific.

**Look:** 2D dynamic, locked Village Painterly look: 6-head characters from the village-cast rig (smooth tapered limbs, detailed 4-finger hands, nose below the eye line), in period costume (Castilian doublets and morions, Aztec and Inca dress, sailors, miners, 1890s uniforms). Highly detailed painted backgrounds with warm light and dynamic cast shadows: Seville's harbour, Tenochtitlan on its lake, the Andes, the red mountain of Potosi, galleons in storms, El Escorial, Havana harbour at night. Moving cameras, 3 to 5 parallax layers, characters acting in every shot, match-cut transitions. SFX under the VO; no music until combine.

## Fact-check fixes against the transcript

- Potosi: transcript says it produced '80 percent of the world's supply'; that figure is not reliable. Script says 'a huge share'.
- Columbus sailed with about 90 men; landfall 12 Oct 1492 (Guanahani, named San Salvador).
- Tordesillas signed 7 June 1494 between Spain and Portugal (built on papal bulls of 1493); transcript wrongly makes the Pope the treaty's broker.
- Tenochtitlan population estimates vary (roughly 150,000 to 250,000); script says 'perhaps two hundred thousand'.
- Cortes scuttled (sank or beached) most of his ships rather than burning them.
- Moctezuma died 30 June 1520 (circumstances disputed); Noche Triste 30 June/1 July 1520. Script avoids saying who killed him.
- Smallpox killed Cuitlahuac, Moctezuma's successor, in late 1520.
- Tenochtitlan fell 13 Aug 1521 after a siege of about 75-93 days.
- Cajamarca 16 Nov 1532, about 168 Spaniards; Atahualpa executed July 1533.
- Transcript calls Pizarro's 1528 trip 'the first expedition'; skipped.
- Manila galleon route ran 1565-1815.
- Philip II's bankruptcies (suspensions of payments): 1557, 1560, 1575, 1596.
- Armada about 130 ships; around 67 returned (estimates vary); script says 'perhaps half'.
- Spain's Habsburg inbreeding and Charles II: kept factual, no medical claims beyond 'sickly' and 'no children'.
- Utrecht 1713: Gibraltar ceded to Britain (Menorca returned later).
- Ayacucho 9 Dec 1824; Mexico independent 1821.
- USS Maine: 15 Feb 1898, 266 deaths; the cause is still debated (later studies suggest an internal coal-bunker fire). Script says 'nobody proved what caused it'.
- Manila Bay 1 May 1898; Santiago de Cuba 3 July 1898; Treaty of Paris 10 Dec 1898, $20 million for the Philippines; Guam also ceded.
