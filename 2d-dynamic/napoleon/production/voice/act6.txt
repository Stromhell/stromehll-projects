This time, the British sent him to Saint Helena, a speck of rock in the South Atlantic, nearly two thousand kilometres from Africa.

He lived in a damp house called Longwood, dictating his memoirs, arguing with his guards, and gardening.

He died on the fifth of May 1821, aged fifty-one, most likely of stomach cancer. His last words were reported as: France, the army, Josephine.

What he left still matters. His legal code of 1804 shaped the laws of dozens of countries. He spread the idea of equal citizens across Europe.

The price was huge too. His wars killed somewhere between three and a half and six million people.

Napoleon had everything. He could have stopped. But there was always one more crown, one more war. In the end, he was left with an island and the sea.
