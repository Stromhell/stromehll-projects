Now Europe knew he could be beaten. Russia, Prussia, Austria, Sweden and Britain joined together. At Leipzig in 1813, about half a million soldiers fought for three days, and Napoleon lost.

In April 1814, with enemies in Paris and his own marshals refusing to fight, he gave up his throne at Fontainebleau.

They sent him to rule the tiny island of Elba. He lasted less than a year.

In March 1815 he landed in France with about a thousand men. The king's soldiers blocked the road. Napoleon walked toward them alone and opened his coat. 'If any of you wants to kill his emperor, here I am.'

Nobody fired. They cheered and joined him. Three weeks later he was back in Paris, and the king had fled.

On the eighteenth of June 1815, near the village of Waterloo, he attacked Wellington's British-led army. Hour after hour, Wellington's line held. Then the Prussians arrived on his flank.

He sent in the Imperial Guard. At close range, the British line stood up and fired. The Guard fell back, and the whole French army broke.
