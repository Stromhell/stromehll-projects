State media now calls her 'respected daughter', and has used titles that South Korean intelligence reads as signs of a successor.

But none of it is official. Her name and age have never been confirmed by North Korea.

And there's a twist. In North Korea, children take their father's family name. If she rules and has children, the Kim name may not pass on.

Maybe she is the next leader. Maybe she is a softer face for a hard state. In a country where the leader writes the rules, only one man decides, and he isn't saying.
