North Korea has passed power from father to son twice. When Kim Jong Il died in 2011, his son was in his late twenties and almost unknown.

He had only months of public preparation. To take control, he had to push aside powerful generals and relatives.

He knows how dangerous a late handover can be. And his own health is a worry. Intelligence reports describe heavy smoking, drinking and weight gain, and the heart problems that ran in his family.

Some experts think showing the daughter is simply good propaganda: a softer image of a family man at a time of shortages after years of closed borders.
