On the second of January 1492, a king rode out of the most beautiful palace in Europe and handed its keys to his enemies.

His name was Muhammad the Twelfth. The Spanish called him Boabdil. He was the last Muslim ruler in Western Europe.

Legend says that on a mountain pass he turned for one last look at Granada and wept. And his mother told him: weep like a woman for what you could not defend like a man.

That moment ended a story that began seven hundred and eighty-one years earlier, when a small army crossed a narrow strip of sea from Africa.

In those centuries, the cities of Muslim Spain had street lamps, public baths and libraries, while most of Europe had none of them.

So how did a few thousand soldiers take a whole country? How did it become the richest place in Europe? And why did it all fall apart?
