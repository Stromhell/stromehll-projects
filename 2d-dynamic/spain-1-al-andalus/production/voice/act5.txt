By 1250 only one Muslim state was left: the Emirate of Granada, a strip of mountains and coast in the far south.

It survived for almost two hundred and fifty years. It paid tribute to Castile, played the Christian kings against each other, and built the Alhambra.

In 1469 two teenagers married in secret. Isabella, heir to Castile, and Ferdinand, heir to Aragon. Together they would rule almost all of Spain.

In 1482 they started a war to finish the Reconquista. Granada was split. The young prince Boabdil had rebelled against his own father, the sultan.

Then Boabdil was captured by the Castilians. Ferdinand let him go, in return for help. So Granada fought a civil war while the Christian armies took its towns one by one.

By 1491 the Christian army camped outside the city and built a whole new town of stone just to show they were never leaving. They called it Santa Fe, holy faith.

Granada was starving. In November 1491 Boabdil signed a surrender treaty. It promised that Muslims could keep their mosques, their laws, their language and their property.

On the second of January 1492, the cross and the royal banners were raised over the Alhambra. Seven hundred and eighty-one years after Tariq landed, Muslim rule in Iberia was over.
