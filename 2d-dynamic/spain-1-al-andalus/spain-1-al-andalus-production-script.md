## Fact-check fixes against the transcript

- Transcript 6 auto-captions garbled names ('Siberia' for Iberia, 'Rodeways' for Roderic, 'Kumawat' for Umayyad); all rewritten from scratch.
- Tariq's army: sources give ~7,000, later reinforced to ~12,000; script says 'about seven thousand'.
- 'Burned his boats' is a later legend; labelled as such.
- Count Julian's daughter story (in the Hindi source) is a later legend and left out.
- Covadonga date traditionally 718 or 722; script says 'about 722'.
- Tours/Poitiers 732 (Charles Martel) kept brief.
- Abd al-Rahman I escaped 750, landed 755, emir 756; Mezquita begun c. 785.
- Cordoba population estimates vary widely (100,000 to 500,000); script says 'a few hundred thousand' with 'historians think it may have'.
- Library of 400,000 volumes is the chroniclers' figure; flagged on screen.
- Abbas ibn Firnas glide (c. 875) comes from a later account; script says 'is said to'.
- Caliphate abolished 1031; taifas 'more than twenty' (counts vary 20-30).
- Santiago sacked 997; bells carried to Cordoba and returned in 1236.
- Granada surrender treaty signed 25 Nov 1491; handover 2 Jan 1492. 781 years counts from 711.
- Alhambra Decree signed 31 Mar 1492; numbers expelled are debated (estimates 40,000 to 100,000+); script says 'tens of thousands'.
- Capitulations of Santa Fe signed 17 April 1492.
- Cisneros book burning in Granada, 1499 (date often given as 1499/1500).
- Morisco expulsion 1609-1614, about 300,000.
- Spanish words of Arabic origin: commonly cited as about 4,000; script says 'thousands'. 'Ojalá' from 'law sha' Allah' is the usual etymology.
- The Hindi source says 750 years; the English source says 781; script uses 781 (711 to 1492).
