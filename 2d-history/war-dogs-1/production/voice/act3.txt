David Packouz has known Efraim since they were kids, sneaking out of synagogue to shoot hoops. Now he's in his twenties, a licensed massage therapist who wants to be a rock star.

December 2005, in a car on the way to a party, Efraim opens his laptop and shows David his bank balance: 1.8 million dollars. He's 19.

David is in. He works on commission, from a one-bedroom flat with an ocean view, glued to the phone and the website from morning to night.

By most accounts they're often high while they work. The press will later call them the stoner arms dealers.

Then Efraim sells weapons to Nepal's king, who is fighting rebels demanding democracy. David hates it. But his girlfriend is pregnant, and he needs the money. He stays.
