Back in Miami he takes over a dormant company his father owned, called AEY, so it looks like an established business. He's too young to hold the licences himself.

His office is a one-room flat. His tool is a public government website called FedBizOpps, where the military posts what it wants to buy.

Anything from toilet seats to rocket launchers. Here's the game. The Army wants 20,000 helmets. You find a factory that sells them for 2.2 million, add shipping, bid 2.7 million, and keep the difference.

The trick is to bid lower than everyone else. On the phone he can sound like a gruff ex-Marine. In 2004, by his own count, he wins over a million dollars of contracts. He's 18.

Bigger deals need cash up front. A Utah businessman in his sixties, Ralph Merrill, agrees to put up the money in return for half the profits.
