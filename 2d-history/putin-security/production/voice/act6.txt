Where does this level of caution come from? Part of the answer may be his past. Putin was a KGB officer in East Germany.

He watched that state collapse in 1989 as crowds took to the streets. Many biographers think it left him deeply distrustful of crowds and uprisings.

Today, much of his inner circle still comes from the security services.

He also knows how many people want him gone: critics he has jailed, rivals, and a country he invaded.

Remember the very long tables he used during meetings in 2022? Guests sat metres away.

His meals are reportedly cooked by his own chefs with their own ingredients, and tested before he eats.

Reports even claim his guards collect his waste on foreign trips, so no other country can study his health.

The way his system works makes access to him the real currency. Rozhdestvensky says those who can reach him hold power, and those who can't hold none.

Ukraine's former military intelligence chief, Kyrylo Budanov, has claimed Putin uses body doubles who have had surgery to look like him. The Kremlin denies it, and there is no public proof.

A former officer of his protection service, Gleb Karakulov, who fled Russia in 2023, described something simpler: decoy runs.

A motorcade drives to a plane, someone who looks a bit like Putin climbs aboard, and the plane flies to Moscow. The real Putin stays where he is.
