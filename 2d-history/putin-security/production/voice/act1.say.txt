Since two thousand, Putin has spent much of his time at Novo-Ogaryovo, a guarded estate in the forest west of Moscow.

He also spends long stretches at a lakeside residence at Valdai, between Moscow and St Petersburg.

There is a summer residence at Sochi on the Black Sea, and a huge palace on the coast at Cape Idokopas that investigators link to him. He denies owning it.

Having many homes is useful. But to get between them, or to meet other leaders, he has to travel.

And a leader on the move is a leader at his most exposed.

He is guarded day and night by uniformed officers and by plain-clothes agents you would never notice.

But the Kremlin's security leans on one idea above everything else: nobody should be able to predict where he will be.
