In 2023, Prime Energy arrived. Same name. Same bright colours. But inside each can, two hundred milligrams of caffeine.

That's roughly two strong cups of coffee. The can said not for under-18s. But the bottle next to it on the shelf was the one kids queued round the block for.

In July 2023, US Senator Chuck Schumer stood at a podium and asked the Food and Drug Administration to investigate Prime's marketing to children.

That same month, Canada recalled cans of Prime Energy found in its shops, because they broke its legal caffeine limit.

Logan's response at the time was that the can was clearly labelled eighteen plus, and the caffeine was industry standard. Years later, he was still annoyed that a senator had spoken about it on a Sunday morning.

But the senator didn't invent the caffeine. It was printed on the can. Putting it in a can that looked just like the kids' favourite drink? That was a choice.
