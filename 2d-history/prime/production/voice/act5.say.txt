Then came the lawsuits. Lots of them. Some Prime started, some it didn't. Let's go through the big ones.

In twenty twenty-four, Lionel Messi launched his own drink, called Mass Plus. Prime said the bottles looked too much like theirs. Messi's side went to court first, and Prime counter-sued.

Logan joked he'd drop it if Messi fought him in the ring. Messi didn't. In January twenty twenty-six, both sides agreed to drop their claims, and the case was dismissed.

Boxer Ryan Garcia posted that Prime was full of dangerous chemicals. In May twenty twenty-four, Prime sued him for defamation. By the end of the month, Garcia had apologised.

A U S class action claimed Prime contained so-called forever chemicals. Prime said that wasn't true, and a judge threw out most of the claims in September twenty twenty-four.

The U S Olympic committee said special Kevin Durant bottles used Olympic marks without paying for them. That one was settled.

And the biggest one came from its own supplier. Ruh-fresco, a bottling giant, says it built a whole production line just for Prime, and Prime walked away before ordering a single bottle. It's suing for nearly sixty-eight million dollars. Prime disputes it.

Think about that. A brand that said it was booming, and a bottling line built for it, standing silent.
