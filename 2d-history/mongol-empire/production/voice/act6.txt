In China, Genghis's grandson Kublai became Great Khan in 1260, after a four-year war with his own brother.

In 1271 he declared a new Chinese-style dynasty, the Yuan, and built a capital at what is now Beijing, called Khanbaliq.

By 1279 he had defeated the Song dynasty in the south, and for the first time all of China was ruled by Mongols.

Foreign visitors came to his court. The Venetian Marco Polo later claimed he served Kublai for seventeen years.

Kublai wanted Japan too. In 1274 he sent a fleet. It landed, fought, and then pulled back, and a storm wrecked many ships on the way.

In 1281 he sent an even bigger invasion, by traditional count more than a hundred thousand men. For weeks the Japanese held them on the beaches.

Then a typhoon struck and destroyed much of the fleet. The Japanese called it kamikaze, the divine wind.

His armies also failed in Vietnam and Java. Even the Mongols had limits at sea and in tropical jungle.
