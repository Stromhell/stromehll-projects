In the 1250s the new Great Khan, Mongke, sent his brother Hulagu west to finish the conquest of Persia and the Middle East.

Baghdad had been the seat of the Abbasid caliphs for five hundred years, with famous libraries and scholars from across the Islamic world.

Hulagu demanded that the caliph al-Mustasim surrender. The caliph refused, and his army was too small to fight.

In January 1258 the Mongols surrounded the city. On the tenth of February it surrendered.

The sack lasted about a week. Estimates of the dead range from tens of thousands to many times that. Nobody knows the true number.

Chroniclers wrote that so many books were thrown into the Tigris that the river ran dark with ink.

The caliph was put to death. One story says he was rolled in a carpet and trampled by horses, because the Mongols avoided spilling royal blood.

Then they moved on toward Syria and Egypt. In 1260, Mongke died, and Hulagu took most of his army back east.

The force he left behind met the Mamluks of Egypt at Ain Jalut, in today's Israel, in September 1260. The Mamluks won.

It was one of the first big battles the Mongols lost in the field, and their push into Africa ended there.
