In the twelfth century, the land we now call Mongolia was split between rival tribes: Mongols, Tatars, Keraits, Merkits, Naimans.

They raided each other for horses, cattle and wives. Alliances lasted until the next betrayal.

Around 1162, a boy named Temujin was born into a noble Mongol family. Legend says he came into the world clutching a blood clot in his fist.

When he was about nine, his father Yesugei was poisoned by Tatars he had shared a meal with.

With the chief dead, the clan packed up and rode away, leaving his widow Hoelun and her children behind.

They survived on roots, wild onions, marmots and fish. Hoelun kept them alive and taught them who their father had been.

In his teens Temujin was captured by a rival clan and locked in a heavy wooden collar called a cangue. One night he escaped and hid in a river.

A family from that clan found him and, instead of handing him over, hid him in a cart of wool. He never forgot people who were loyal to him.

He married Borte, the girl he had been promised to as a boy. Soon after, raiders from the Merkit tribe kidnapped her.

To get her back he went to his father's old ally Toghrul, and to his childhood blood-brother Jamukha. Together they crushed the Merkits.
