In Central Asia, a Turkic-Mongol warlord named Timur, also called Tamerlane, tried to rebuild the empire in the late 1300s.

He was not descended from Genghis, so he married a Genghisid princess and called himself the Khan's son-in-law.

His armies sacked Delhi in 1398 before he went home to Samarkand.

A century later, his descendant Babur lost his kingdom in Fergana and looked south toward India. Through his mother, he was also descended from Genghis Khan.

In April 1526, at Panipat, Babur's army of perhaps twelve thousand men beat the much larger army of the Delhi Sultan Ibrahim Lodi.

He used cannons and matchlocks, carts chained together, and Mongol-style riders sweeping round the sides.

People in India called Babur's dynasty the Mughals, from the Persian word for Mongol. Their empire lasted, in some form, until 1857.
