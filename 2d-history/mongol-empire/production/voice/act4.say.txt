Genghis Khan died in 1227 while fighting the Western Xia kingdom. Nobody knows exactly how, or where he is buried.

His third son Ogedei became Great Khan, built a capital at Karakorum, and sent the armies further than ever.

In 1236 his nephew Batu and the old general Subutai rode west. They crossed the Volga and took Russian cities in the depth of winter, using frozen rivers as roads.

In December 1240 they took Kyiv, then the greatest city of the Rus.

In April 1241 two Mongol armies struck Europe at once: one in Poland, one in Hungary.

On the ninth of April, at Legnica, they beat an army of Polish and German knights.

Two days later at Mohi, by the Sajo River, the Hungarian king camped inside a ring of wagons. The Mongols surrounded it, left one gap open, and cut down the men who fled through it.

Western Europe had no army in the field that seemed able to stop them.

Then, in December 1241, Ogedei died, far away in Mongolia. When the news arrived, Batu turned his armies back east.

Historians still argue about why they never came back. Politics at home was part of it. Some think poor grazing land in Hungary was part of it too.
