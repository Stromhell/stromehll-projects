By 1215 the Mongols had taken Zhongdu, the Jin capital that is today Beijing. To the west lay the Khwarazmian Empire, rich and powerful, ruled by Shah Muhammad.

Genghis wanted trade, not war. In 1218 he sent a caravan of several hundred merchants loaded with silver, silk and furs.

At the border town of Otrar, the governor accused them of being spies. He had them killed and took their goods.

Genghis sent envoys to demand justice. The Shah executed one of them and sent the others back with their beards shaved off.

In 1219 the Mongols came, with an army that may have been somewhere between one and two hundred thousand men.

They split into several armies and hit city after city: Otrar, Bukhara, Samarkand, Urgench.

Cities that opened their gates were usually spared. Cities that resisted were often destroyed, and their people killed or taken as captives.

Engineers captured in China built siege engines. Prisoners were driven ahead of the army as human shields.

The Shah ran. He died in late 1220 on a small island in the Caspian Sea, sick and nearly alone.

His son Jalal ad-Din fought on and fled to the Indus River in 1221, where Genghis caught him. He escaped by riding his horse off the riverbank.

The Mongols turned back before going deeper into India. Persian chroniclers wrote that whole regions lost much of their population.
