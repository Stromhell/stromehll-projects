Then every NATO convoy hits the same bottleneck: the Suwałki Gap, a strip of land along the Polish-Lithuanian border.

It is only about 65 kilometres wide. On one side is Kaliningrad, a heavily armed piece of Russia. On the other is Belarus, Russia's closest ally.

Missiles, drones and artillery from both sides could turn the roads into a gauntlet. If the gap closes, the Baltics can only be reached by sea or air.

That makes the Baltic Sea vital, and one island most of all: Gotland, owned by Sweden, a NATO member since 2024.

Experts call it an unsinkable aircraft carrier. Jets from Gotland could hit Russian supply lines, and Sweden says it could be ready for action within days.
