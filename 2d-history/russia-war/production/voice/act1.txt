In Belgium sits SHAPE, NATO's military headquarters. Its commander, the Supreme Allied Commander Europe, gets the call: Tallinn, Riga and Vilnius are under attack.

Russia's likely plan is called a fait accompli. Grab the land fast, dig in, and dare NATO to start a much bigger war to take it back.

In a famous war game by the RAND think tank, Russian forces reached the Baltic capitals in about 36 to 60 hours.

The three Baltic states are small. Together they have about six million people, and they border Russia or its ally Belarus on almost every land side.

So every hour they hold out matters. Each day of delay gives NATO time to arrive.
