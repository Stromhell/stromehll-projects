Over the Baltics, the fight for the sky has already started. Russia sends its most advanced fighters, S U fifty-sevens.

At a German airbase, two Eurofighter Typhoons scramble. They can be in the air within 15 minutes and reach the eastern flank in about an hour.

But much of the war happens lower down, with drones. They are cheap, and the missiles used to shoot them down often cost far more.

Russia builds drones on a huge scale. Recon drones like the Orlan ten spot targets for hours, then guide in the attack drones.

The Geh-rahn two, Russia's copy of Iran's Sha-hed drone, can fly more than one thousand kilometres and crash into its target with a large warhead.

The Baltics are arming themselves with drones too. One Lithuanian officer has said cheap drones could do most of the damage to an invading force.
