Back on the ground, the tanks unload at Bremerhaven. Now they must cross Germany by train.

The route east runs along the North Sea-Baltic corridor, one of Europe's main transport arteries. But much of Germany's rail network is old and overloaded.

Then comes a tiny problem: 8.5 centimetres. Baltic railways still use the wider Soviet-era gauge, so trains can't simply roll straight through.

A new standard-gauge line, Rail Baltica, should fix that. It was due in 2026. The target is now 2030, and some fear even later.

And in our scenario, saboteurs strike. Shadowy figures set fire to a railway signal box. The military train has to stop until the signals work again.

Some countries fence off their railways. Germany relies mostly on cameras and drones, and its rail operator says the network is simply too big to guard everywhere.
