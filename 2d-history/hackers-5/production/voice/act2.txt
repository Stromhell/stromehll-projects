For him it's a challenge, not a heist. He breaks in to prove a system isn't as secure as it thinks. Sometimes he emails the admin afterwards to point out the hole.

Summer 1999. He gets into NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center in Alabama, onto 13 computers.

He downloads software NASA valued at 1.7 million dollars, part of the space station's environment controls. He later said the code was poor, and he only wanted to study it.

Having the code didn't let him control the station. But NASA shut its systems down for three weeks to check them.

Weeks later he gets into a server of a Pentagon agency that deals with weapons threats, and reads thousands of staff emails.
