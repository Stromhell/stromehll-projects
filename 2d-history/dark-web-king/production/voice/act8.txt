His trial opened in Manhattan in January 2015.

His lawyers argued he had created Silk Road as an experiment, handed it over to others, and was lured back to take the fall.

But the strongest evidence was his own laptop: chat logs, records, and the journal where he wrote about building the site.

On the fourth of February, 2015, the jury found him guilty on all seven counts, including drug trafficking, money laundering and running a criminal enterprise.

At sentencing, prosecutors linked six overdose deaths to drugs bought on Silk Road. Two parents who lost sons spoke. The defence disputed those links.

Ross asked for mercy. He told the judge he wished he could go back and take a different path.

On the twenty-ninth of May, 2015, Judge Katherine Forrest sentenced him to two life terms plus forty years, with no possibility of parole.

She called what he'd done terribly destructive to society, and said the sentence had to warn anyone thinking of following him.

His appeals failed. In 2018, the Supreme Court declined to hear his case.

He was thirty-one. By law, he would die in prison.
