Then the story turns darker. This part needs care, because it was never proven to a jury as a criminal charge.

In January twenty thirteen, a federal task force from Maryland arrested a Silk Road staff member named Curtis Green, at his home in Utah.

Soon after, about twenty thousand bitcoins disappeared from Silk Road accounts, taken with Green's login.

Roberts blamed Green. According to prosecutors, he asked a trusted contact called Nob to have Green killed, and paid eighty thousand dollars.

But Nob wasn't a smuggler. Nob was Carl Force, an undercover agent with the Drug Enforcement Administration.

Force staged the killing. Green, who was cooperating, posed for photos covered in fake blood, and the pictures were sent to Roberts as proof.

Weeks later, prosecutors say, a user called FriendlyChemist demanded five hundred,000 dollars, or he would publish the names of thousands of Silk Road users.

Chat logs show Roberts paying about 150,000 dollars in bitcoin to another user to kill the blackmailer.

Canadian police found no such person and no such murder. Investigators believe it was a scam.

In all, prosecutors alleged he paid around 730,000 dollars for at least five killings. No one was killed.

He was charged over the Green plot in Maryland, but the charge was dropped in twenty eighteen. He was never convicted of murder-for-hire, though a judge weighed it at sentencing.
