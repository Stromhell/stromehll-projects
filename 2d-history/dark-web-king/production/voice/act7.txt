So the plan was to catch him with the laptop open, logged in as Roberts.

On the afternoon of the first of October, 2013, Ross walked into the Glen Park branch of the San Francisco Public Library.

He sat at a table by the window and opened his laptop on the free Wi-Fi.

Around him, agents dressed as ordinary library users waited.

Outside, an undercover Homeland Security agent who had worked his way onto Silk Road's staff chatted with Roberts and asked him to check something on the site.

Ross logged in.

Behind him, two agents posing as a couple started a loud argument.

Ross turned to look.

In that second, an agent slid the open laptop away and passed it to a colleague. It was still unlocked.

On the screen, he was logged in to an account called mastermind.

He was handcuffed among the bookshelves. Silk Road was taken offline the next day.

On the laptop, agents found his journal, records of the business, and about 144,000 bitcoins.
