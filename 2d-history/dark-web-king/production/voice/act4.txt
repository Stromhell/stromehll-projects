In February 2012, the site's boss posted: I need a name. My new name is Dread Pirate Roberts.

It came from the film The Princess Bride. In the story, the Dread Pirate Roberts isn't one man. The name is handed down, so the legend never dies.

The message was clear. Even if you catch me, someone else could be Roberts.

Ross moved around. He spent time in Australia and abroad, then in 2012 settled in San Francisco.

He rented a room, paid in cash, and told his housemates his name was Josh.

Online, Roberts wrote long posts about freedom and a world without government. He even ran a book club for users.

And the business grew. By the autumn of 2013, there were nearly thirteen thousand drug listings.

Prosecutors said that from early 2011 to mid-2013, the site handled about nine and a half million bitcoins in sales, from nearly 147,000 buyer accounts and almost 3,900 sellers.

At 2013 prices, the FBI put that at about 1.2 billion dollars. Silk Road's own cut was around eighty million.

At the prices of the day, it was smaller, about 183 million dollars. Either way, nothing like it had been seen online.

To the agents hunting him, Roberts was a ghost. No face, no address, no real name.
