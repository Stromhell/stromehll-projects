Meanwhile, the hunt had stalled. Top cyber investigators were trying to crack the site's technology.

The breakthrough came from a tax investigator in New York: Gary Alford of the IRS.

His method was simple. He searched Google for the earliest mentions of Silk Road.

He found a post from January 2011 on a mushroom forum. A user called altoid was telling people about a new site called Silk Road.

Then the same username on a Bitcoin forum, in October 2011. Altoid was hiring a programmer, and said to email rossulbricht at gmail dot com.

A real name. The pirate's mask had cracked.

In July 2013, border officers seized a package from Canada with nine fake IDs, all with Ross's photo, addressed to his San Francisco home.

When Homeland Security agents knocked, he told them that, hypothetically, anyone could buy fake IDs on a site called Silk Road.

Around the same time, the FBI said a leak in the site's login page pointed to its server, in Iceland.

Investigators quietly copied it. How exactly they found it was later disputed in court.
