You'd think the biggest drug dealer on the internet was a gangster.

He wasn't. He was a twenty-six-year-old science graduate from Texas with a laptop and an idea.

His website let thousands of dealers sell to customers around the world, hidden from the police and paid in a new kind of digital money.

By the FBI's count, it handled more than a billion dollars in sales in under three years.

He called himself the Dread Pirate Roberts. The FBI, the DEA, the IRS and Homeland Security all wanted to know who he was.

In the end, it came down to one old forum post, and one second in a public library.

In twenty fifteen, a judge gave him two life sentences plus forty years, with no chance of parole.

Ten years later, he walked out of prison a free man. How?

And here's the twist: two of the federal agents chasing him ended up in prison too.
