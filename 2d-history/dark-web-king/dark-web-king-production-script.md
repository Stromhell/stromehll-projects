# Dark Web King

Working title: **He Built the Amazon of Drugs at 26. Then He Got Two Life Sentences.**

11 parts (cold open + 10 acts), 128 shots, 2281 words, about 16:07 at 160 wpm.

**Running device:** An 'ONION METER' in the corner: a small painted glass onion with five glowing layers, one for each of Ross's protections: TOR, BITCOIN, ALIAS, SERVER, LAPTOP. It builds up layer by layer in Acts 1-4, then the investigators peel one layer at a time (the alias in Act 6 when the Gmail post turns up, the server when Iceland is found, the laptop in Act 7 when it is grabbed open) until only the bare core is left. An OPEN LAPTOP on a library table recurs in the cold open, Act 7 and the final shot, where its lid finally closes.

**Look:** Tense, clear true-crime documentary in the house 2D cutout style, using the ALIUM character look from the BlackRock video: skin-tone oval heads, soft hair with a light streak, dark irises under heavy upper lids, rosy cheeks, no nose, slim long limbs with fingered hands, rolled cuffs and sneakers, clothes in a muted, slightly desaturated palette, warm dark-plum ink lines. Characters sit inside the scene with contact shadows, rim light and reflected colour; Disney-like animation with anticipation, squash and stretch, overlapping action on hair and clothes, eased holds and clear silhouettes. Behind them, highly detailed painterly backgrounds with dynamic shadows and highlights: a sunlit Austin, Texas street in 2010; a cluttered bedroom desk at 3 a.m. lit only by monitor glow; a wooden cabin in the Texas hills; the Bondi Beach promenade at dusk; a foggy San Francisco street of painted Victorian houses; the Glen Park branch library in slanting afternoon light through tall windows; a Manhattan task-force office piled with case files under fluorescent strips; a cold blue Reykjavik server hall; a wood-panelled Manhattan federal courtroom; a desert prison fence at dusk in Arizona; a Las Vegas conference stage in 2025. Internet ideas are painted as objects: a huge iceberg under a night sea, a glass onion with glowing layers, parcels riding mail-sorting conveyors, a public ledger as an endless paper scroll. Drugs and drug use are never shown: only plain sealed parcels and text tiles. No weapons shown. Real people are Alium-style stylised cutouts, never photo likenesses; websites and companies are plain text tiles, no logos. SFX under the VO (keyboard clicks, server hum, library hush, mail sorters, gavel); no music until combine.

## Fact-check fixes against the transcript

- Caption/name errors fixed: 'Ross Ulb/Ulbr/Ulbrit/Ulbrick' = Ross Ulbricht; 'Dreet Pirate Roberts' = Dread Pirate Roberts; 'Critis Green' = Curtis Green; 'Karl/Call Force' = Carl Force; 'Sh. Burgess/Bridges' = Shaun Bridges; 'Gaukar' = Gawker; 'Gary Alfred' = Gary Alford; 'Iceland data centre' kept (FBI's account).
- Pardon date corrected: the transcript says the news broke in September 2025 via the New York Times. Trump announced the full and unconditional pardon on 21 January 2025 (his second day in office) on Truth Social, 'in honor of' Ulbricht's mother and the Libertarian movement; Ulbricht left a federal prison in Arizona that evening after more than 11 years. Trump had promised it at the Libertarian National Convention in May 2024.
- Agencies corrected: Carl Force was a DEA agent, not FBI; Shaun Bridges was a Secret Service agent, not FBI. Both were on the Baltimore (Maryland) Silk Road task force.
- Force: as 'French Maid' he sold Ulbricht investigation information for about $100,000 in bitcoin (DOJ). Transcript's '$57,000' could not be confirmed and is replaced. Sentenced to 78 months (Oct 2015), DOJ release confirms.
- Bridges: used Curtis Green's admin login to divert about 20,000 bitcoins (about $350,000 at the time), later cashed out for about $820,000. Sentenced to 71 months in December 2015 (a further 24 months were added in a later case; not mentioned in VO). The transcript says Green's credentials were used by 'the FBI' to steal $350,000; corrected to Bridges personally.
- Order of events corrected: Green was arrested in January 2013 and the 'Nob' hit on him came first; the FriendlyChemist $500,000 blackmail came after, in March 2013 (the transcript puts the blackmail first).
- Murder-for-hire, worded with care: the transcript's '$80' is $80,000 (paid to 'Nob', i.e. Force, for the Green hit, per the Maryland indictment). FriendlyChemist hit: about 1,670 BTC (~$150,000) to 'redandwhite'; Canadian police found no such person and no such murder. Prosecutors alleged about $730,000 in total for at least five killings; no one was killed. Ulbricht was never charged with murder-for-hire in New York; the Maryland count was dropped in July 2018 after the NY conviction became final. VO says 'prosecutors say/alleged', 'chat logs show', 'never proven to a jury', 'never convicted'.
- Sales figures from the criminal complaint: 9,519,664 BTC in sales, 614,305 BTC in commissions, 146,946 buyer accounts, 3,877 vendors, 6 Feb 2011 to 23 Jul 2013. The $1.2B and ~$80M figures are at 2013 exchange rates; at the prices of the day sales were about $183M (and ~$13M commission). Transcript's '$1.2 billion of trade in 2013' (it's cumulative over 2.5 years) and 'trillions of dollars at today's prices' dropped.
- Listings: ~13,000 drug listings in late 2013 (complaint). Transcript's '13,000+ products and services of every kind' narrowed to drug listings.
- Transcript implies you could buy anything incl. weapons and citizenship; Silk Road banned stolen credit cards and child abuse material among other things. VO says most listings were drugs, with fake IDs and hacking tools also sold. 'Citizenship' and 'hack anyone's bank account' claims dropped.
- Launch date: transcript says January 2011. The site went live in early (February) 2011; altoid's first forum post about it was in late January 2011. VO says 'early 2011' / FEB 2011 on screen. Ulbricht was born 27 March 1984, so 26 at launch (hook verified); 29 at arrest; 31 at sentencing.
- Bio: born Austin, Texas; Eagle Scout; BS physics, UT Dallas (full scholarship); MS materials science and engineering, Penn State (2009). Ran a used-book business (Good Wagon Books) before Silk Road. Mushrooms grown in a cabin come from his own journal entered at trial.
- Transcript says he moved to his sister in Sydney in November 2011 and took the DPR name then. Sources confirm he spent time in Sydney/Australia and abroad but not the date or the sister link; the DPR name was adopted on 5 February 2012 ('I need a name... My new name is Dread Pirate Roberts'). VO softened to 'spent time in Australia and abroad, then in 2012 settled in San Francisco'.
- Dread Pirate Roberts is from The Princess Bride (William Goldman's novel; 1987 film): a title passed between successive pirates. Verified.
- Iceberg: the '4% surface / 96% hidden' split is a widely repeated but unmeasured claim. VO says nobody can really measure it, only that the deep web is far bigger than what search engines index.
- Tor: developed at the US Naval Research Laboratory in the mid-1990s to protect government communications (transcript said 'for spies'). It routes through three relays (guard, middle, exit) chosen from thousands of volunteer relays; transcript's 'Pakistan → Brazil → Germany → Russia, every step erased' simplified to three relays and layered encryption. Using Tor is legal in most countries; buying illegal goods is not.
- Bitcoin: launched January 2009 by 'Satoshi Nakamoto'. Transcript calls it untraceable; corrected: transactions are on a public ledger (pseudonymous, not anonymous), which later helped catch Force and Bridges.
- Gawker article (Adrian Chen) published 1 June 2011; Senator Chuck Schumer's press conference demanding a shutdown followed days later (5 June 2011). Verified.
- Gary Alford (IRS-CI, New York) found 'altoid' posts: Shroomery forum, late January 2011, promoting Silk Road; Bitcointalk, October 2011, hiring an 'IT pro' with contact rossulbricht@gmail.com. Verified.

In San Francisco he rented a room for cash and went by 'Josh'.
- Server: the FBI said a misconfigured login/CAPTCHA leaked the server's IP, located in Reykjavik, Iceland, and imaged with Icelandic help in July 2013. The defence disputed the account; VO says 'the FBI said... later disputed in court'.
- Arrest: 1 October 2013, Glen Park branch, San Francisco Public Library (one CBS/AP report says 2 October; most sources and court records say 1 October). HSI agent Jared Der-Yeghiayan, undercover as Silk Road staff, chatted with him and got him logged in; two FBI agents staged a lovers' quarrel; an agent slid the open laptop away and handed it to a colleague. Logged-in account: 'mastermind'. Site seized 2 October 2013. ~144,000 BTC seized from his laptop.
- Trial: opened 13 January 2015, Manhattan federal court; guilty on all seven counts on 4 February 2015. Defence argued he founded the site, handed it off, and was lured back as the fall guy.
- Sentence: 29 May 2015, Judge Katherine B. Forrest: two life terms plus 40 years (concurrent), no parole; ~$183M forfeiture. Prosecutors cited six overdose deaths; two parents spoke; the defence disputed causation. Judge's remarks paraphrased ('terribly destructive to our social fabric'). Second Circuit affirmed May 2017; Supreme Court denied cert 28 June 2018.
