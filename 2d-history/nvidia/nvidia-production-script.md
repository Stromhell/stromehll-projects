# How Jensen Huang Actually Built Nvidia

Working title: **He Built the Wrong Chip and Still Built the World's Most Valuable Company**

10 parts (cold open + 9 acts), 79 shots, 1537 words, about 10:42 at 160 wpm.

**Running device:** A 'DAYS OF CASH LEFT' counter in the corner that keeps nearly hitting zero (NV1 flop, Sega deal, RIVA 128), then flips into a market-value counter that climbs to $5 trillion.

**Look:** Calm serious tech-history documentary in the house 2D style. Painted plates: 1970s Kentucky boarding school, a 1990s Denny's booth under fluorescent light, a cramped early-90s office, a Tokyo boardroom, server halls glowing green. Chips as 3D props (NV1 board, RIVA 128, GeForce 256, H100). Real people as round-headed ink characters; company names as plain text, no logos.

## Hooks (yt-script hookscore)

- (55) A dishwasher from Denny's runs the first $5 trillion company in history. The chip that almost killed it is the reason you should never trust a sure thing.
- (38) Nvidia built the wrong chip, lost its only customer, and was 30 days from going out of business. Then Jensen Huang flew to Tokyo and asked the client he was quitting for $5 million.

## Fact-check fixes against the transcript

- Added (not in transcript): six-month product cycle, 3dfx asset purchase (Dec 2000), 2008 laptop-chip defect charge (~$196M) and share-price fall, DGX-1 hand-delivered to OpenAI (2016), CUDA software lock-in, $1T (2023), $3T (2024), $4T (July 2025) milestones, ~60 direct reports.
- '$5 empire' fixed to $5 trillion; Nvidia passed $5T market value on 29 Oct 2025.
- '$20 every single hour' is garbled; dropped.
- Founding: April 1993 (transcript said the pitch was in 1992; the idea was discussed in late 1992). Co-founders Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem named; transcript left them out.
- Oneida: Jensen's uncle thought it was a prestigious school; it was a strict Baptist boarding school that took troubled kids. He cleaned bathrooms. Kept factual, no 'reform school' label.
- Sega: the contract was for Sega's next console chip (NV2). Sega's Dreamcast ended up using another company's graphics chip, so 'the Dreamcast' is dropped. CEO Shoichiro Irimajiri agreed to pay the remaining $5M as an investment; Jensen says it kept Nvidia alive about six months.
- RIVA 128: 1997, about 1 million units in four months. Nvidia used an emulator to save time. 'About a month of payroll' matches Jensen's own accounts.
- 'Wrote one word on the whiteboard: More' dropped: not sourced. Replaced with his real catchphrase, 'Our company is thirty days from going out of business'.
- GeForce 256 (1999) was marketed as the world's first GPU. Nvidia IPO January 1999.
- CUDA: announced 2006. The '$500 million' figure isn't solidly sourced; script says it was expensive and barely earned money for years.
- AlexNet 2012: 15.3% top-5 error vs 26.2% for the runner-up; trained on two GTX 580 cards. Team named (Krizhevsky, Sutskever, Hinton).
- Arm: announced September 2020, $40B; FTC sued December 2021; UK and EU investigated; deal abandoned February 2022. The UK did not formally block it, so 'the UK blocked it' is changed.
- China: A100/H100 export ban in 2022; H800 made to fit the rules; blocked again in October 2023.
- Closing quote is 'my will to survive exceeds almost everybody else's will to kill me' (transcript dropped 'almost').
