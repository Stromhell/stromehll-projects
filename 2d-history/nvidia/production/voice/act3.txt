Nvidia had one lifeline: a contract with Sega to build the graphics chip for its next game console.

But that chip was based on the same curved-surface design, the one the rest of the industry was abandoning.

If they finished it, they'd spend a year building something dead. If they quit, they'd lose their only paying customer.

So Jensen flew to Japan and did something unusual. He told Sega's chief executive, Shoichiro Irimajiri, that Nvidia's approach was wrong, and that Sega should find another partner.

Then he asked for the rest of the money anyway. As an investment, not a payment, because without it Nvidia would go under.

Irimajiri agreed. Jensen has said the only reason was that Irimajiri liked him.

That five million dollars bought Nvidia about six months.
