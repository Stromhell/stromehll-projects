By the early nineties, Jensen and two engineers from Sun Microsystems, Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem, shared an idea.

Video games were moving to 3D, and ordinary computer processors couldn't keep up. Someone would build a chip just for graphics.

They founded Nvidia in April 1993, with about forty thousand dollars between them.

Their first chip, the NV1, came out in 1995. It tried to do everything at once: graphics, sound and game controller support on one card.

It also drew 3D shapes in an unusual way, using curved surfaces called quadratic patches.

Then Microsoft released DirectX, the standard most PC games would be built on. And DirectX was built around triangles.

Nvidia had bet the company on the opposite approach. Jensen later said they built the wrong thing with great confidence.

The NV1 sold badly. Money was running out.
