Here's the difference that changed everything. A normal processor is like one very smart worker doing tasks one after another, very fast.

A G P U is like thousands of simple workers, each doing one small calculation at the same time.

For graphics, that's perfect: every pixel can be worked on at once. But lots of other problems look the same. Weather. Chemistry. Physics.

In two thousand six Nvidia announced Kooda, software that let scientists program its graphics chips for any kind of calculation.

It was expensive. Every chip had to carry extra hardware for it, and for years, hardly anyone paid for it.

Wall Street didn't like it. Nvidia's share price fell hard after two thousand eight. Jensen kept funding Kooda anyway, and gave it to universities.

In two thousand eight things got worse. A manufacturing defect in some laptop chips forced Nvidia to set aside about two hundred million dollars, and the financial crisis hit at the same time.

From its two thousand seven peak, the stock lost more than three quarters of its value. Investors asked why Nvidia was spending so much on a product almost nobody used.
