A man who washed dishes at Denny's as a teenager now runs the first company in history worth five trillion dollars.

It started in 1993, in a booth at a Denny's in San Jose, where three engineers drank refill coffee and argued about a chip that didn't exist yet.

Within a few years, their company built the wrong chip, lost almost everything, and came within about a month of running out of money.

The chip that nearly killed it is the reason it won. And the bet that made it a giant earned almost nothing for six years.

This is the story of Nvidia, and of Jensen Huang, a man who later said his will to survive exceeded almost everybody else's will to kill him.
