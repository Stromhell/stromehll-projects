In nineteen ninety-nine, Nvidia went public, and launched the Jee Force two fifty-six.

It marketed the chip with a new name: the G P U, the graphics processing unit.

The Jee Force took over three D calculations that used to fall on the computer's main processor. Games got smoother, lighting got better, worlds got bigger.

By the early two thousands, Nvidia was the leading name in PC graphics. Gamers, game studios and film effects houses all ran on it.

Nvidia also won the deal to supply the graphics chip for Microsoft's first Xbox in two thousand one.

Nvidia won partly through speed. Jensen pushed the team to ship a new generation of chips roughly every six months, while rivals took a year or more.

Its biggest rival, three D F X, the maker of the Voodoo cards that gamers had loved, couldn't keep up.

At the end of two thousand, Nvidia bought what was left of three D F X. Five years after the N V one flop, Nvidia was the company others were trying to catch.
