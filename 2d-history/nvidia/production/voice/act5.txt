In 1999, Nvidia went public, and launched the GeForce 256.

It marketed the chip with a new name: the GPU, the graphics processing unit.

The GeForce took over 3D calculations that used to fall on the computer's main processor. Games got smoother, lighting got better, worlds got bigger.

By the early 2000s, Nvidia was the leading name in PC graphics. Gamers, game studios and film effects houses all ran on it.

Nvidia also won the deal to supply the graphics chip for Microsoft's first Xbox in 2001.

Nvidia won partly through speed. Jensen pushed the team to ship a new generation of chips roughly every six months, while rivals took a year or more.

Its biggest rival, 3dfx, the maker of the Voodoo cards that gamers had loved, couldn't keep up.

At the end of 2000, Nvidia bought what was left of 3dfx. Five years after the NV1 flop, Nvidia was the company others were trying to catch.
