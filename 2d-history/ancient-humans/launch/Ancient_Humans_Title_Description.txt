Title: They Found Food in 15 Hours a Week. Where Did Your Time Go?

Tags: ancient humans, hunter gatherers, prehistoric life, 15 hour work week, Richard Lee, Kalahari, Ju/'hoansi, persistence hunting, Blombos Cave, Hohle Fels flute, Polly Wiessner, first sleep second sleep, agricultural revolution, original affluent society, history documentary, animated history


Description:
Hunter-gatherers in the Kalahari found their food in about fifteen hours a week. So why do you, surrounded by machines built to save time, have so little of it? This is one ordinary day fifty thousand years ago, and what happened to all those free afternoons.

We start with two skeletons from the same region: one tall and healthy, one worn down, and the farmer is the one you wouldn't guess. Then we walk through a hunter-gatherer day: a slow morning, a persistence hunt, Richard Lee's stopwatch in the Kalahari (and the fair version, about 40 hours once chores count), shell beads and a 40,000-year-old bone flute, friendship as insurance, firelight stories and the "first and second sleep." Finally, the farming loop that shut the door behind us and gave us hours that belong to someone else.
