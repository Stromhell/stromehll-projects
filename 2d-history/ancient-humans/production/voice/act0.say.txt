This morning, a machine screamed at you until you got up.

You silenced it, and from that second the day was already spoken for. Where to be. What to finish. How many hours belong to someone else.

Now go back fifty thousand years.

A man wakes with the light. No alarm, no shift, no one waiting for him.

He owns a spear, two hides and a fire. And he gets to ask a question most of us never get to ask on a weekday morning.

What do I feel like doing today?

Your brain is already pushing back. Ancient life was short, brutal and hungry, wasn't it? Every hour a fight to stay alive.

For a long time, that was the version in every textbook. Then scientists started counting, and digging, and the story cracked.

Which leaves a strange question. You live surrounded by machines built to save time. Washing machines, microwaves, cars.

So how did people with none of that end up with more free afternoons than you? And where did your hours go?
