When the fire burns low, people sleep. But maybe not the way you do.

Historian Roger Ekirch found hundreds of old European records that mention a first sleep and a second sleep, with a waking hour in between.

People would wake around midnight, lie quietly, think, pray or talk, and then sleep again.

And when a 1990s experiment kept volunteers in long winter nights with no artificial light, many slipped into the same split pattern.

Not every study agrees. When researchers tracked sleep among the Hadza, the San and the Tsimane, most slept in one block.

So if you wake at two in the morning, it may not be a disorder. Your body may just be doing something very old.
