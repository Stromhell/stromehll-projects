Some hunters in Africa have been recorded using a method that looks nothing like the movies. They don't try to outrun the animal. They can't.

Instead they track it, at a steady pace, for hours, in the midday heat.

A human can sweat and keep cooling while moving. A four-legged runner has to stop to pant. Push it hard enough, for long enough, and it overheats.

Eventually the animal stops and can't run again. The hunters walk up to it.

You were never the fastest thing on the plain. You were the thing that could keep going.

This is called persistence hunting, and it's only one method among many. Most hunts used traps, snares, ambush and poisoned arrows.

A big hunt can take the whole day, and it's the hardest work this life asks for. But one good kill feeds the camp for days.
