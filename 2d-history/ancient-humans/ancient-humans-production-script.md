# What Did Ancient Humans Actually Do All Day?

Working title: **They Found Food in 15 Hours a Week. Where Did Your Time Go?**

10 parts (cold open + 9 acts), 97 shots, 1812 words, about 12:40 at 160 wpm.

**Running device:** A running 'HOURS THAT ARE YOURS' clock in the corner: it shows a full free afternoon for the hunter-gatherer, then shrinks act by act as farming arrives, ending on the viewer's alarm.

**Look:** Calm serious documentary. Warm firelight palette for the camp, cold blue-grey for the modern office and the alarm. Painted savanna and Kalahari plates, parchment maps, 3D props (spear, hide, skeleton bones, shell beads, flute, plough, grain sacks, alarm clock).

## Hooks (yt-script hookscore)

- (41) Your alarm went off this morning and your day stopped being yours. In 1963 a scientist timed people who still hunt and gather: 15 hours a week to find food. What are you paying for the other 25?
- (40) Everyone says cavemen worked every waking hour just to survive. You are the one who does. In 1963 a man with a stopwatch timed hunters: 15 hours a week.

## Fact-check fixes against the transcript

- '17 hours a week' (transcript): Lee's 1960s food-getting figure is usually cited as about 12 to 19, average about 15 hours a week. Script uses ~15 and calls it food only.
- Transcript leaves out that Lee's later total, adding tool-making, food processing and chores, was about 40 to 44 hours a week. Added, so the video isn't misleading.
- 'Hadza, Aché, Martu all came back 15 to 20 hours' is not supported: Aché men spent much longer hunting. Script says the numbers vary and some groups spend far longer.
- 'Lives than' (missing word) rewritten: 'better fed and less worn down'.
- Skeleton height: cited as about 15 cm fall in men's average height in Greece/Turkey after farming (Angel 1984). Said 'in parts of', not 'everywhere'.
- Blombos beads: about 75,000 years old (transcript said 'over 70,000'); shells came from estuaries some kilometres away, not 'a long walk to the coast'.
- Hohle Fels flute: vulture wing bone, five finger holes, about 35,000 to 40,000 years old; found 2008. Kept as 'around forty thousand'.
- 'James Suzman: 6 hours a day of social time': couldn't find a source. Dropped.
- Wiessner (PNAS 2014): 81% of long night-time conversations were stories. Correct; the name is spelled Wiessner (transcript had 'Weisner').
- Two sleeps: historical European records (Ekirch) and the 1992 Wehr experiment support it, but Yetish et al. 2015 found Hadza, San and Tsimane mostly sleep in one block. Added the caveat.
- Persistence hunting: presented as one documented method among many, not how all hunting worked.
- Farming start: about 12,000 years ago in the Fertile Crescent (transcript said 10,000).
