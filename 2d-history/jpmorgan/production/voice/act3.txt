In March 2000 he got his chance: chief executive of Bank One in Chicago, a big bank in poor shape.

He bought about two million Bank One shares with his own money, close to sixty million dollars. He later said it was about half of everything he had.

Then he went through the bank line by line. He cut costs, cleaned up bad loans and tightened the checks on risk.

Within a few years, Bank One was making billions a year again.

In January 2004, JPMorgan Chase agreed to buy Bank One for about fifty-eight billion dollars. Part of the deal was that Dimon would one day run the combined bank.

He became president, and on the last day of 2005 he took over as chief executive. A year later he was chairman too.

His rule was simple. Hold more capital than rivals, keep piles of cash ready, and plan for the worst year, not the average one.

He called it a fortress balance sheet. To see why that mattered, you need to know how a bank actually makes money.
