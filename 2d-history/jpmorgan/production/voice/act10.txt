In 2024, JPMorgan made a record fifty-eight and a half billion dollars. In 2025, it made fifty-seven billion.

By the end of 2025, it held about four point four trillion dollars in assets.

Dimon is now seventy and still running the bank, while the race to succeed him narrows.

And he is still warning. In October 2025 he told the BBC he was far more worried than others about a serious fall in the stock market within the next two years.

On the boom in artificial intelligence, he said it would pay off overall, the way cars and TVs did, but some of the money being poured in will probably be lost.

So, three lessons. One: in a crisis, cash is the most valuable thing you can own. The bank with cash gets to buy.

Two: judge a business by its worst year, not its best. Ask what happens if your three biggest fears come true at once.

Three: when everything looks too good, read the data. In 2006, the warning was sitting in a monthly report on late payments.

Bank B always looks smarter, right up until the year it disappears.

In 2008, an eighty-five-year-old bank was sold for two dollars a share. The buyer didn't win by being the cleverest in the boom. It won by being ready for the bust.
