In 2025, JPMorgan Chase made fifty-seven billion dollars in profit.

Bank of America, the second-biggest bank in the country, made about thirty and a half billion. Citigroup made about fourteen.

Put those two together, and they still come up more than ten billion dollars short of JPMorgan on its own.

And it is not a lucky streak. In 2008, the worst financial crisis since the Great Depression, JPMorgan still made a profit while Lehman Brothers went bankrupt and Citigroup lost billions.

That same year it bought one of Wall Street's oldest investment banks over a single weekend, for two dollars a share.

The man behind it all had been pushed out by his own mentor ten years earlier, after a weekend company retreat.

His name is Jamie Dimon. His big idea sounds almost boring: keep more money aside than anyone thinks you need. He calls it a fortress balance sheet.

In the boom years, rivals made more by taking bigger risks. Then the bust came. This is how one dull-sounding rule built the most powerful bank in America.
