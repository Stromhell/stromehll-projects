When people are frightened, they move their money to the bank they trust most.

JPMorgan's deposits went from about seven hundred and forty-one billion dollars at the end of two thousand seven to more than one trillion by the end of two thousand eight.

For two thousand eight, JPMorgan made a profit of five point six billion dollars. Wells Fargo made about two point seven. Citigroup lost about eighteen point seven billion.

In October two thousand eight, the Treasury had the biggest banks take government capital. JPMorgan took twenty-five billion dollars, and paid all of it back in two thousand nine.

The competition had thinned out. Bear was absorbed, Lehman was gone, Merrill had been sold and Citigroup needed repeated government help.

By two thousand nine, JPMorgan ranked number one in the world for investment banking fees.

In twenty ten, it earned seventeen point four billion dollars, then a record for the bank.

But the deals came with a bill. Bear and WaMu had sold mortgage bonds before the crash that later went bad, and now JPMorgan owned those problems.

In twenty thirteen, JPMorgan agreed to a thirteen billion dollar settlement over mortgage securities sold by itself, Bear Stearns and Washington Mutual, and admitted it had made serious misrepresentations to investors.

Even so, it came out of the crisis bigger and stronger than any of its rivals.
