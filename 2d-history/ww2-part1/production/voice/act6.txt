Then, strangely, almost nothing happened in the west. Britain and France had declared war, but for months they barely fought. People called it the Phony War.

Stalin, meanwhile, attacked Finland in November 1939. Leningrad was only about 30 kilometres from the Finnish border, and he wanted a buffer.

The tiny Finnish army held out for more than three months. In March 1940 Finland gave up about 11 percent of its land, but stayed independent.

On 9 April 1940 Germany struck Denmark and Norway. Denmark fell in hours; Norway fought for two months. Its king and government escaped to London.

In Britain, Chamberlain's policy of giving in had failed. On 10 May 1940 he resigned, and Winston Churchill became prime minister.
