To pay, Germany printed money. By nineteen twenty-three prices were doubling every few days.

In Berlin, a loaf of bread cost about 160 marks in nineteen twenty-two. By November nineteen twenty-three it cost around 200 billion.

People carried cash in wheelbarrows. Savings built over a lifetime were worth nothing.

In this chaos, a young agitator named Adolf Hitler tried to seize power in Munich in nineteen twenty-three. The coup failed and he went to prison, but it made him famous.

He blamed Germany's humiliation on Jews and on communists. It was a lie, but angry, frightened people listened.
