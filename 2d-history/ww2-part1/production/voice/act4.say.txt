In February nineteen thirty-eight Hitler summoned Austria's chancellor, Kurt Schuschnigg, and bullied him into putting Nazis in his government.

Schuschnigg called a vote on independence. Before it could happen, German troops crossed the border. On 13 March nineteen thirty-eight Austria was declared part of Germany.

Next was the Sudetenland, a border region of Czechoslovakia where about three million German speakers lived. Hitler demanded it.

In September nineteen thirty-eight Britain's prime minister, Neville Chamberlain, met Hitler in Munich with France and Italy. Czechoslovakia wasn't even in the room.

They handed him the Sudetenland in return for a promise of no more war. Chamberlain flew home waving the paper and promised 'peace for our time'.

Six months later, in March nineteen thirty-nine, German troops took the rest of Czechoslovakia. The promise was worthless.
