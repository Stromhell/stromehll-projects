Poland was next. To make it safe, Hitler did something shocking: in August 1939 he signed a pact with his sworn enemy, Stalin's Soviet Union.

Secretly they agreed to split Poland between them.

On 1 September 1939, about one and a half million German troops invaded Poland from the west, north and south.

Two days later Britain and France declared war on Germany. Australia, New Zealand, South Africa and Canada soon followed.

Germany used a new way of fighting: tanks smashing through fast, planes bombing from above, no time for the enemy to react. It was called Blitzkrieg, lightning war.

Many German soldiers were given Pervitin, a methamphetamine pill, to keep them awake and fighting for days.

Poland's army still relied heavily on horses. On 17 September the Soviets invaded from the east. By early October, Poland was gone, split in two.
