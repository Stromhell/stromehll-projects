That same day, Germany attacked the Netherlands, Belgium, Luxembourg and France, with around three million soldiers and thousands of tanks and planes.

Group B swept into the Netherlands and Belgium, drawing the best British and French troops north. Group C sat facing the Maginot Line.

Group A was the real plan. Its tanks drove through the Ardennes, a thick, hilly forest French generals thought tanks could never cross.

They broke through at Sedan on 13 May and raced to the sea. The Allied armies in the north were cut off.

Hundreds of thousands of soldiers were trapped on the beaches at Dunkirk with the sea at their backs.

From 26 May, Britain sent everything that could float: navy ships, ferries, fishing boats, pleasure yachts. Fighter planes covered them overhead.

By 4 June about 338,000 soldiers had been rescued. But they left almost all their tanks and guns behind.

Italy's dictator, Mussolini, joined Hitler and declared war on 10 June. On 22 June 1940 France signed an armistice.
