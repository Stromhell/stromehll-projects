Then, strangely, almost nothing happened in the west. Britain and France had declared war, but for months they barely fought. People called it the Phony War.

Stalin, meanwhile, attacked Finland in November nineteen thirty-nine. Leningrad was only about 30 kilometres from the Finnish border, and he wanted a buffer.

The tiny Finnish army held out for more than three months. In March nineteen forty Finland gave up about 11 percent of its land, but stayed independent.

On 9 April nineteen forty Germany struck Denmark and Norway. Denmark fell in hours; Norway fought for two months. Its king and government escaped to London.

In Britain, Chamberlain's policy of giving in had failed. On 10 May nineteen forty he resigned, and Winston Churchill became prime minister.
