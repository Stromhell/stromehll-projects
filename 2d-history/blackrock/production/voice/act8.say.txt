Every January, Larry Fink writes a public letter to the bosses of the companies BlackRock invests in.

In twenty twenty, the letter said that climate risk is investment risk, and that BlackRock would put sustainability at the centre of how it invests.

When the biggest owner in the market says that, every boardroom listens.

Then the backlash came from the other side. Republican-led states called it political, and some pulled money out. Florida alone moved about two billion dollars away in twenty twenty-two.

BlackRock changed its tone. It let more of its fund investors cast their own votes, and in twenty twenty-five it left the climate alliance Net Zero Asset Managers.

Attacked from the left for not doing enough, and from the right for doing too much. That's what happens when you own a slice of everyone.
