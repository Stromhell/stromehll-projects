For decades, the normal way to invest was to hand your savings to a fund manager who picked stocks for you.

You paid a yearly fee, often around one percent or more, whether the manager did well or not.

And most managers didn't beat the market. Over fifteen years, around nine in ten large US stock funds did worse than the S&P five hundred index.

So someone asked an obvious question. If the index is so hard to beat, why not just buy the index?

The S&P five hundred is a list of five hundred of the biggest US companies, each with a weight. Bigger companies get a bigger share.

An index fund copies that list exactly. Put in ten thousand dollars, and a program splits it by those weights. No meetings. No stock picking.

If the market rises five percent, you rise five percent. If it falls, everyone falls with you.

Because there's no star manager to pay, the fee can be tiny. BlackRock's main S&P five hundred E T F charges about three hundredths of one percent.

That's the whole product. Cheap, simple, and very hard to argue with.
