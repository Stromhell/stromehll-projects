Over the next fifteen years, Americans moved trillions of dollars into index funds. A big share of it went to BlackRock.

By 2026, iShares alone held more than six trillion dollars.

Each fee is tiny. But a tiny fee on trillions of dollars, running automatically every day, is an enormous business.

And here's the part that matters. Every dollar that goes into an index fund buys a slice of every company in the index.

That's how BlackRock ends up holding around seven to eight percent of Nvidia, Microsoft and Apple. Nobody at BlackRock chose those stocks. The index did.

It isn't alone. Two other giants, Vanguard and State Street, grew the same way. Together they're called the Big Three.

Researchers at the University of Amsterdam found that, taken together, the Big Three were the largest shareholder in about nine out of ten companies in the S&P 500.

And shareholders get votes. On who sits on company boards, on how executives are paid, on big strategy questions.
