In nineteen eighty-eight, Fink and a small group of partners set up a new firm inside the offices of the private equity company Blackstone.

It was called Blackstone Financial Management. A few years later it took a new name: BlackRock.

The pitch to clients was about protection more than prediction: we will show you what could go wrong with your money before it happens.

To do that, they built software that ran on a single workstation at first. They called it Aladdin.

Aladdin asks 'what if?' across every holding. What if interest rates jump? What if oil collapses? What if borrowers stop paying?

It runs thousands of scenarios and shows how each one would hit a portfolio, loan by loan and bond by bond.

Over time BlackRock began renting Aladdin to other investors: pension funds, insurers, even rival asset managers.

Today it helps watch over trillions of dollars, much of it not BlackRock's own.
