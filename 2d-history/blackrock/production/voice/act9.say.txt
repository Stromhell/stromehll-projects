That voting power is where the trouble starts. In late twenty twenty-four, Texas and ten other states sued BlackRock, Vanguard and State Street.

The states pointed out that the three firms held big stakes in America's largest coal producers, in the power companies that burn coal, and in the tech giants that buy the electricity.

At the same time, the firms had joined climate groups that pushed companies to cut emissions.

The lawsuit argues that this worked like a cartel: owners of competing coal companies using their influence to cut output, which pushed up prices for everyone.

The firms deny it. They say index funds simply own what the index holds and don't run the companies.

In February twenty twenty-six, Vanguard settled for twenty-nine and a half million dollars without admitting wrongdoing. BlackRock and State Street are still fighting the case.

Whoever wins, the case asks a real question. What happens when the same few investors own a slice of every competitor in an industry?
