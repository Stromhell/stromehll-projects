In two thousand eight, the same family of products Fink had helped pioneer blew up the world's banks.

Millions of American mortgages had been pooled, sliced and resold, many of them to borrowers who couldn't keep paying.

When house prices fell, nobody could tell which slices were still worth something and which were worthless.

BlackRock had something almost nobody else had: twenty years of data and a machine built to answer exactly that question.

So the US government hired it. The Federal Reserve and the Treasury brought BlackRock in to value and manage the toxic assets of Bear Stearns and AIG, and to advise on others.

The firm that had grown out of one banker's loss became the firm Washington called to clean up the losses.

It happened again in twenty twenty. When Covid froze the markets, the Federal Reserve hired BlackRock to run its emergency programme for buying corporate bonds.

Some of that buying went into BlackRock's own bond E T Fs, under rules the Fed had approved. Critics noticed.
