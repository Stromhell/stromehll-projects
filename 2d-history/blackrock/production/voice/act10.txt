Index funds have saved ordinary savers huge amounts in fees. A teacher's pension can now own the whole market for almost nothing.

But the same money that makes investing cheap also gathers ownership into a very small number of hands.

It began with a young banker who lost a hundred million dollars because he didn't understand his own risk.

He built a machine to measure risk, used it to win Washington's trust in 2008, and bought the right business at exactly the right moment.

Now the question isn't how BlackRock got so big. It's who should keep watch over a company that owns a piece of everything.
