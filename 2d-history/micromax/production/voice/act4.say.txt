Micromax kept spotting gaps. Most phones were designed for men. A stylish phone aimed at women, the Bling, came with sparkling crystals and a budget price.

At first it barely sold. Distributors said the problem was simple: nobody knew it existed.

So they ran the TV ad, with Akshay Kumar and Twinkle Khanna, right before Karva Chauth. Husbands everywhere suddenly knew what to gift.

Then smartphones arrived. Micromax, along with Lava and Karbonn, went after cheap Android phones. Micromax's Canvas series became the one to have.

Big screens, dual SIMs and big batteries at half the price of a Samsung. Small-town India loved it.

Micromax expanded into tablets, televisions and data cards, and sold phones in Russia and other markets too.

Most of these phones were designed with, and built by, partners in China, then branded and sold by Indian companies. That would matter later.

By twenty fourteen Micromax was on top. Research firm Counterpoint put it ahead of Samsung in Indian phone sales in one quarter. It had Hugh Jackman as its face and sponsored the Asia Cup.

The company that once made booth phones for Nokia now sold more phones in India than Nokia did.
