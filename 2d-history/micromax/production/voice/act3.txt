Rahul travelled through Uttar Pradesh and Bihar to see how people actually used phones.

He found the problem: people had phones, but the power kept going out, and batteries lasted a day or two.

He came back and told his team he wanted to make Micromax's own phone. They thought he was mad. Who could take on Nokia?

In 2008 they launched the Micromax X1i. Its headline feature: a battery that could last up to 30 days on standby.

Rahul got budget for a first batch of about ten thousand phones. If they didn't sell, that was the end of the idea.

Distributors refused to stock it. Customers only ask for Nokia, they said.

Finally an old distributor agreed to try. The first batch, about ten thousand phones, sold out in roughly ten days.

The lesson Micromax took: Indian buyers wanted a phone that solved their problem, at their price, more than a famous name.
