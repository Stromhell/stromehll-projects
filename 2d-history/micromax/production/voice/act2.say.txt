Around two thousand, a mobile phone was a luxury in India. In towns and villages, people made calls from yellow STD-PCO booths.

Those booths ran on landlines: expensive to install, messy wiring, constant repairs.

Micromax's idea: a booth phone that worked on a SIM card instead. Put in a SIM, attach an antenna, and the booth is ready.

They sold these wireless booth phones through telecom operators: first Airtel, then others. Every minute of every call made money for the operator, so they loved it.

Before that, the booth phones had one big customer: Nokia, which bought them to supply its own booth business. Revenue reached about 23 crore rupees.

Then, the story goes, Nokia called. It was getting out of the booth business. Micromax had a factory full of phones and no buyer.

So Rahul went straight to the phone companies themselves.

Revenue climbed past 100 crore rupees. For a while, that was the business.

The family bought a new car. His parents finally believed this computer business might actually work.
