So how did we get here? Rewind to twenty twelve. Tesla launches the Model S with a seventeen-inch touchscreen in the middle of the dash, and the world loses its mind.

The logic sounded clever. Phones got better when they lost their buttons. So surely cars would too?

And every new model came with more features. Massage seats, ambient lighting, twelve drive modes. There simply wasn't room for a button for everything, so the screen swallowed the lot.

But there's one big problem. A button you can feel. A screen you have to look at. And at seventy miles an hour, there's only one thing your eyes should be looking at. The road.

And when the screen dies, things you need die with it. In twenty twenty-one, Tesla recalled about a hundred and thirty-five thousand Model S and Model X cars because the screen's memory chip could wear out, taking the demister and the reversing camera with it.
