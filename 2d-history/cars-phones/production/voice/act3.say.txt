Mercedes, meanwhile, went the other way. Its Em-bucks Hyperscreen is glass from pillar to pillar, with a screen just for the passenger.

Some models even get a dashboard camera for video calls. Yes, you can join the Monday meeting from the driver's seat. And another camera watches your eyes, to check you're not distracted. In a cabin that glows like a nightclub.

So why push screens so hard? The honest answer is money. A good switch costs real engineering: moving parts, wiring, that lovely solid click. A touchscreen is one part that does everything.

And once your car has a screen, it can show you anything. Ford has filed a patent for adverts inside the car, based on where you are and where you're going.

Ford says a patent isn't a plan. But some Jeep owners have already reported full-screen pop-up ads at traffic lights. Don't like the ads? I'm sure there'll be an ad-free subscription.
