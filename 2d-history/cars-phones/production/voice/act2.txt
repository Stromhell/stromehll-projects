In 2022, a Swedish car magazine called Vi Bilägare decided to measure exactly how bad this is.

They took twelve cars, drove each one at about seventy miles an hour, and gave the driver four simple jobs. Change the radio, adjust the heating, reset the trip computer, dim the dashboard.

The winner? A 2005 Volvo V70. Seventeen years old, covered in buttons. All four jobs in ten seconds, about three hundred metres of road.

The slowest was the MG Marvel R, an all-screen electric SUV. Forty-five seconds. Nearly one and a half kilometres of road, driven half blind.

And it gets sillier. In some Teslas, the glovebox opens from a menu on the screen. Even the gears moved there. Swipe up for drive, swipe down for reverse.

Then Volkswagen tried to out-minimal Tesla. It replaced real buttons, even on the steering wheel, with touch-sensitive pads that you could brush by accident.

Owners hated it. And in 2025 VW's design boss, Andreas Mindt, said physical buttons were coming back, and promised they would never, ever make that mistake again.
