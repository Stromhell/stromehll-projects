Even Ferrari got caught up in the screen craze. In May twenty twenty-six, it showed its first fully electric car, the Loo-chay, in Rome. The interior was designed with Johnny Ive's studio. Yes, the man behind the iPhone.

The reaction was brutal. Ferrari's shares fell about eight percent in Mi-lan, billions wiped off in a day. One former Ferrari boss reportedly called it a historic mistake.

En-zo Ferrari liked to say he sold engines, and the car came free. The Loo-chay has no engine at all. Somewhere, En-zo is spinning.

Porsche learnt the hard way too. After betting big on electric, its operating profit for twenty twenty-five fell by about ninety-three percent.

And what did Porsche show next? The Mission S concept. A mid-engined supercar with a real key, real buttons, round dials with a proper needle, and no central screen. Need sat nav? There's a holder for your phone.

So maybe there's hope. Let carmakers build phones on wheels if they want. But we already have phones. When buyers stop paying for them, watch how fast the buttons come back.
