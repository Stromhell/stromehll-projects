Which brings us to the sneakiest idea of all. Paying a subscription for things your car already has.

In twenty twenty-two, B M W owners in Britain found they could pay about fifteen pounds a month to switch on their heated seats. The heaters were already in the seat. You were renting the button.

People were so angry that B M W dropped it a year later. But the idea didn't die.

Mercedes sold extra acceleration on its electric E Q S and E Q E in America for twelve hundred dollars a year. Same motors, same battery. The power was there all along, locked behind a paywall.

And when the software stops being supported, the car can lose features you paid for. When America switched off three G in twenty twenty-two, early Teslas needed a two-hundred-dollar modem upgrade to stay connected. Some Hondas faced bills of around nine hundred.

That's the phone business model. Rent you the features, then make the old one feel ancient. The difference is, a phone costs a few hundred pounds. A car costs a lot more.
