April twenty nineteen. Key Lar-go, Florida. Just after nine at night, a Tesla Model S is cruising on Autopilot. The driver drops his phone, and reaches down to find it.

The road ends at a T-junction. The car doesn't stop. At over sixty miles an hour, it runs straight through and hits a parked S U V. A young couple are standing beside it.

Twenty-two-year-old Nye-bell Ben-ah-vee-dess was killed. Her boyfriend was badly hurt. In twenty twenty-five, a jury in Miami decided the driver was mostly to blame, but that Tesla was one third responsible too. The bill: around two hundred and forty-three million dollars.

Here's the strange bit. Holding your phone at the wheel in Britain can cost you six points and a two-hundred-pound fine. But a carmaker bolting a fifteen-inch tablet to your dashboard, and telling you the car can drive itself? Apparently that's fine.

So did Tesla turn the car into a phone? And is that why some of them keep crashing?
