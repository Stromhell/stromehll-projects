Picture this. A wet Tuesday on the M sixty-two. Seventy miles an hour, lorries throwing spray, and your windscreen starts to steam up.

In a car from two thousand five, you don't even look down. Your hand finds the big chunky button by feel. Click. Done. Eyes never leave the road.

Now try it in a brand-new car. Wake the screen. Find the climate menu. Swipe. Hunt for a demist icon the size of a Tic Tac. Then hit a pothole and jab the wrong thing.

Here's the funny bit. Holding your phone at the wheel can cost you six points and a two-hundred-pound fine. But a carmaker bolting a fifteen-inch tablet to your dashboard? Apparently that's fine.

So how did your car turn into a phone? Who decided this? And why are some carmakers already quietly backing out?
