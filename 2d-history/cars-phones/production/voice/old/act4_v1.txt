But the scariest part isn't the screen. It's the doors. Most Teslas don't open with a simple lever and a cable. They open with a button, powered by electricity. A phone, basically, with a door attached.

After a bad crash, that electric power can fail. There are manual releases inside, but they're often hidden, unlabelled, and hard to find in a panic. Bloomberg found at least fifteen deaths where people couldn't get a Tesla door open after a crash.

In November 2024, a Cybertruck crashed and caught fire in Piedmont, California. Three students died. Police said speed and impairment caused the crash. But their families are suing Tesla, claiming the doors trapped them inside.

Regulators have opened investigations into Tesla door handles, and US lawmakers are pushing for a rule that every car must have a mechanical door release anyone can find. Tesla's design chief says it's working on combining the two.

That's the problem with treating a car like a phone. When a phone crashes, you restart it. When a car crashes, you need to get out.
