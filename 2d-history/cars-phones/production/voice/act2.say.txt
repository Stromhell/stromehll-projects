In twenty twenty-two, a Swedish car magazine called Vee Bee-lair-gah-reh decided to measure exactly how bad this is.

They took twelve cars, drove each one at about seventy miles an hour, and gave the driver four simple jobs. Change the radio, adjust the heating, reset the trip computer, dim the dashboard.

The winner? A two thousand five Volvo V seventy. Seventeen years old, covered in buttons. All four jobs in ten seconds, about three hundred metres of road.

The slowest was the M G Marvel R, an all-screen electric S U V. Forty-five seconds. Nearly one and a half kilometres of road, driven half blind.

And it gets sillier. In some Teslas, the glovebox opens from a menu on the screen. Even the gears moved there. Swipe up for drive, swipe down for reverse.

Then Volkswagen tried to out-minimal Tesla. It replaced real buttons, even on the steering wheel, with touch-sensitive pads that you could brush by accident.

Owners hated it. And in twenty twenty-five VW's design boss, Andreas Mint, said physical buttons were coming back, and promised they would never, ever make that mistake again.
