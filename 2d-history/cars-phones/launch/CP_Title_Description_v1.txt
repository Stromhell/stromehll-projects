TITLE
Tesla Turned Cars Into Phones. Now They Keep Crashing

DESCRIPTION
In 2019, a Tesla on Autopilot ran straight through a junction in Key Largo while the driver reached for his dropped phone. A young woman was killed, and in 2025 a Miami jury decided Tesla was one third responsible: around $243 million. In Britain, holding your phone at the wheel gets you six points and a £200 fine. So why did carmakers bolt a fifteen-inch tablet to the dashboard?

This is how the touchscreen ate the car: the 70 mph test a 2005 Volvo won, the glovebox hidden in a menu, the Autopilot name problem, the doors that won't open after a crash, the in-car adverts, heated seats you pay for monthly, Ferrari's iPhone-designer EV, and the carmakers now bringing the buttons back.

Sources: Vi Bilägare (2022 touchscreen test), NHTSA (Autopilot investigation and recalls), California DMV (Autopilot marketing ruling), court reporting on Benavides v. Tesla (Tesla is appealing), Bloomberg (door investigation), Autocar (Volkswagen), Porsche and Ferrari results reporting. Lawsuits in this video are claims unless a court has ruled.
