And Jason makes the third offer himself. Use the files, he says. The deal was only for you. At least one of us can make it out. Lucia's answer: we're in this together, all the way.

Two choices appear on screen. Swim for it, or No Surrender. Here's where the endings split.

No Surrender. Lucia says, he's going to be OK, J. They kiss, and drive straight at the police line. Over the credits: a quiet street, and a woman hugging a small child. The game never says who she is.

But remember the savings account. Abe said if anything happened to them, the money would go to the boy. That jar, the one that filled and emptied all game, might be the last thing they leave him.

Swim for it can go two ways. In the bad version, Jason gets out holding a gun and the police open fire. They never reach the water.

In the other, Jason says: don't draw your weapon. Hands up, they walk to the edge. Three, two, one, jump. Lucia goes over. Jason is tackled to the road before he can follow.

Over the credits, Jason is in prison. Long hair, full beard, orange jumpsuit. A guard says he has a visitor. Behind the glass is Lucia, with a little boy who presses his hand to the window. Surprise, she says.

The boy isn't named. But if it's Tommy, it's the first time Jason has ever seen his son. Which swim ending you get is decided earlier, by how you played: their relationship, how much is in that savings account, and how much needless violence you caused.

After every ending, the same final scene. Cal, waiting all day for the files to open. At night, they finally do, and he says: no freaking way. The game never shows what he saw.

So this story starts with a lie, he's my boyfriend, and ends on the truest line in it: we're in this together, all the way. Everyone outside sold them. Mattia, Valentina, Abe, Gallo. Three times Lucia was offered a way out without him. She never took it.
