When Jack went live, he went to Kick. A platform launched in twenty twenty-two that sold itself to creators on one big promise. Fewer rules.

But even Kick had a line. Weeks before what came next, after reports of its streamers harassing people at TwitchCon, several accounts were suspended.

Then, on the fifth of October twenty twenty-four, Jack went live from the driver's seat of a roughly two-hundred-thousand-dollar McLaren. On a wet highway near Miami.

According to NBC News, the stream appeared to show him glancing down at the phone in his hand. Moments later, the car veered into a guardrail.

He posted the wreck himself. Crashed my McLaren, glad we're both okay. Viewers noticed he seemed more upset about the car than his cameraman, who needed stitches in his head. Jack later apologised to him.

Kick banned him within hours, saying it doesn't condone illegal activity. And a Florida Highway Patrol crash report, obtained by the Miami New Times, gave him a citation for using his phone while driving.

Days later he was live on TikTok, asking viewers to send him gifts, and explaining that he'd simply hydroplaned. Pouring rain. A McLaren. What did people expect?
