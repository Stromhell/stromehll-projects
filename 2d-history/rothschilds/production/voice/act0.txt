London, November 1875. The British government has a chance to buy a huge stake in the new Suez Canal, the shortcut to India. It needs four million pounds, fast.

Parliament isn't sitting, so it can't vote the money. Prime Minister Disraeli sends his secretary to one man.

Lionel de Rothschild, the story goes, asked one question: 'What is your security?' 'The British government.' 'Then you shall have it.'

One family could lend a superpower four million pounds almost overnight. How did they get there?
