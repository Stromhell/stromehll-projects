The family put money into more than loans. In 1853 Nathaniel bought the Bordeaux vineyard now called Château Mouton Rothschild. In 1868 James bought Château Lafite.

They had terrible timing. A tiny insect, phylloxera, was about to destroy most of France's vineyards. The family had the money to ride it out. Many growers didn't.

They backed mines too. The London and Paris houses helped finance Cecil Rhodes in merging the South African diamond fields into De Beers in 1888.

In the 1880s the Paris branch bought into oil around Baku on the Caspian Sea, and for a while was one of the biggest players in the world oil trade, up against Rockefeller's Standard Oil.

They sold out in 1911, to Royal Dutch Shell, for shares.

And there were eccentrics. Walter Rothschild filled a museum with hundreds of thousands of specimens, rode a giant tortoise, and drove a carriage pulled by zebras to Buckingham Palace.
