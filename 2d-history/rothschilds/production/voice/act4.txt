Through the 1800s, if a government needed a big loan, the Rothschilds were often the first call. Britain, France, Austria, Prussia, the new kingdom of Belgium, the new empire of Brazil.

In Paris, James financed the early French railways. Trains carried passengers on lines his bank had paid for.

When Belgium broke away from the Netherlands in 1830, the new country needed money to exist. The Paris and London houses lent it.

And when Brazil split from Portugal, Nathan's bank handled the loans that let Brazil pay Portugal off and get its independence recognised.

In London, Nathan's bank lent the British government much of the 15 million pounds it borrowed in 1835 to pay off slave-owners when slavery was abolished. The enslaved people got nothing.

In 1847, Lionel de Rothschild helped found the British Relief Association, which raised money for Ireland during the Great Famine.

Lionel also fought for 11 years to take his seat in Parliament without swearing a Christian oath. In 1858 he became the first practising Jewish MP.

In the Crimean War in 1854, the British government borrowed 16 million pounds through Lionel's bank to fight Russia.

In Vienna, Salomon funded Austria's first big railway, and later the family helped prop up the Habsburg empire's finances through wars and revolutions.

After France lost the war with Prussia in 1871, it owed Germany five billion francs. The Rothschilds helped raise the loans that paid it off early.

Not everything worked. The Naples branch faded after Italy unified in 1861, and the family that had been everywhere slowly closed it.

And then, in 1875, the Suez Canal loan that opened our story. Seven years later, Britain was in control of Egypt.
