Description:
In November 1875 Britain had days to buy a huge stake in the Suez Canal and needed four million pounds. Parliament wasn't sitting. Disraeli sent his secretary to one man, Lionel de Rothschild, and the money came almost overnight. This is how one family got to the point where it could lend a superpower that much, and what is myth about them.

It starts in 1744 on the Judengasse in Frankfurt, a walled street where Jewish families were locked in at night. Mayer Amschel Rothschild trained at a Hanover bank, traded rare coins, and became banker to Prince William of Hesse-Kassel. He sent his five sons to Frankfurt, Vienna, London, Naples and Paris. Through the 1800s they financed railways, new countries, Britain's 1835 slave-owner compensation, the Crimean War and France's war payments. Then came vineyards, diamonds, Baku oil, and Walter Rothschild's zebras. We also cover the antisemitic myths built around the name, what the family lost under the Nazis, and why online claims of "trillions" are fantasy.
