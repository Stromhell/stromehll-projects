But money doesn't buy everyone. In early nineteen seventy-nine, the Shah of Iran, the mightiest king in the region, is overthrown.

Then, on the twentieth of November nineteen seventy-nine, during dawn prayers, armed men seize the Grand Mosque in Mecca and call for the fall of the House of Sah-ood. The siege lasts two weeks, and hundreds die.

The Saudis crush the rebels. Then, quietly, they adopt much of their agenda. They slow down Westernisation and hand the clerics more power. Bend, don't break.

With revolutionary Iran next door, the six kings form a club. In May nineteen eighty-one, they found the Gulf Cooperation Council. On paper, a defence pact. In practice, kings protecting kings.

Then, at about two in the morning on the second of August nineteen ninety, Saddam Hussein sends around a hundred thousand troops into Kuwait. The emir escapes, and Kuwait is declared Iraq's nineteenth province.

An American-led coalition throws Iraq out. The ground war lasts just a hundred hours, and the Al Sah-bah family is back on the throne.

And here's the big shift. America takes over Britain's old job as bodyguard. Except this time, the kings pay for it. Kat-ar's Al Oo-dade becomes the biggest American base in the Middle East.
