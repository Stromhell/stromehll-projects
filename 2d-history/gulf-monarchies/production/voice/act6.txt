2011. The Arab Spring. Within a year, the rulers of Tunisia, Egypt, Libya and Yemen are gone.

The Gulf's first answer is cash. Saudi Arabia pledges about a hundred and thirty billion dollars for jobs, wages and housing. Oman creates tens of thousands of public jobs. And the rich neighbours pledge ten billion dollars each to Oman and Bahrain.

The second answer is force. In Bahrain, a Shia majority ruled by a Sunni royal family fills the Pearl Roundabout from the fourteenth of February. At first, they want a constitutional monarchy, not the end of the king.

On the fourteenth of March, about a thousand Saudi troops and five hundred Emirati police cross into Bahrain under the G C C banner. Martial law follows, and the Pearl monument is bulldozed.

That's the whole system in one picture. Money where there's enough of it, neighbouring kings where there isn't, and outside protectors who look the other way.
