1952. Army officers topple Egypt's king. Suddenly, monarchy looks like a leftover from the colonial age.

Then July 1958, Iraq. Another British-backed kingdom. Brigadier Abdul Karim Qasim seizes power in a single day. The young King Faisal the Second is killed, and Iraq is a republic by nightfall.

In 1961, Kuwait's protection deal with Britain ends, and Qasim claims Kuwait belongs to Iraq. Britain rushes in troops, and Iraq backs off. Lesson learned. Small states survive because someone bigger stands behind them.

North Yemen's Imam falls in 1962. And in 1965, a rebellion in Oman's Dhofar region becomes a Marxist insurgency, armed by China and the Soviet Union.

Then, in January 1968, Prime Minister Harold Wilson drops a bombshell. Britain, short of money after devaluing the pound, will pull its forces out of the Gulf by the end of 1971.

A year later, Gaddafi topples Libya's king. The bodyguard is packing his bags, and revolution is next door.
