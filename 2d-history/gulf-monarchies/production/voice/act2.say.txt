Then July nineteen fifty-eight, Iraq. Another British-backed kingdom. Brigadier Abdul Ka-reem Kah-sim seizes power in a single day. The young King Fay-sal the Second is killed, and Iraq is a republic by nightfall.

In nineteen sixty-one, Kuwait's protection deal with Britain ends, and Kah-sim claims Kuwait belongs to Iraq. Britain rushes in troops, and Iraq backs off. Lesson learned. Small states survive because someone bigger stands behind them.

North Yemen's Ee-mahm falls in nineteen sixty-two. And in nineteen sixty-five, a rebellion in Oh-mahn's Dho-far region becomes a Marxist insurgency, armed by China and the Soviet Union.

Then, in January nineteen sixty-eight, Prime Minister Harold Wilson drops a bombshell. Britain, short of money after devaluing the pound, will pull its forces out of the Gulf by the end of nineteen seventy-one.

A year later, Ga-daffy topples Libya's king. The bodyguard is packing his bags, and revolution is next door.
