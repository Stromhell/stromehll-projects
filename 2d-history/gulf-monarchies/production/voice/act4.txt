Britain leaves in 1971. Bahrain and Qatar become independent, the emirates join together as the U A E, and then comes the jackpot. Between 1972 and 1982, oil goes from about three dollars a barrel to about thirty.

So the rulers offer their citizens a deal. No income tax. A well-paid government job, sooner or later. Free healthcare, free education, cheap power, even land.

Today, around two in three working Saudis, and roughly nine in ten working Emiratis, are employed by the state.

The price? No real say in who runs the country. No taxation, no representation. The old American slogan, flipped on its head.

But somebody has to build the towers and run the ports. So the rulers bring in millions of foreign workers. And after the boom, they switch from Arab workers to South Asians, partly out of fear that Arabs would bring revolutionary politics with them.

Remember the pearl captain's rule? It grew into kafala, the sponsorship system that ties workers to their employers. Today, foreigners are about four in ten people in Oman, and close to nine in ten in Qatar and the U A E.

So the people doing most of the work can't vote, can't organise, and can be sent home. And the citizens on top have far too much to lose to start a revolution.
