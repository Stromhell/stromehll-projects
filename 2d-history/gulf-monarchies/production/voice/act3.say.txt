Now for the strangest move in the whole story. Oh-mahn's Sultan Sah-eed bin Tie-moor is sitting on oil money, and spending almost none of it.

By nineteen seventy, roughly six hundred and seventy thousand Oh-mah-nees have just two small hospitals and a handful of schools. And his own son, Ka-boose, trained at Sandhurst, is kept under virtual house arrest.

On the twenty-third of July nineteen seventy, Ka-boose overthrows his father in a palace coup, with British officers helping. The old sultan is flown out on an R A F plane, and he never comes back.

The family stays. Only the king changes. Two years later, Kat-ar does its own version. Sheikh Khalifa pushes the emir aside while he's abroad, without a shot fired.

Ka-boose does what his father wouldn't. He spends the money on his people, brings in help from Britain, Iran and Jordan, and gives defeated rebels jobs in government. By the mid-seventies, the insurgency is beaten.

That's the secret rule of the Gulf. The family works like a company. Lose a boardroom fight, and you line up behind the winner. Because a civil war means everyone loses everything.
