# Why Gulf Kings Never Fall (GM) production script

Working title: Iran Fired Thousands of Missiles at Them. Not One Crown Fell

Source: YouTube reference (Versed, 'Why Gulf Monarchies Never Collapse', youtu.be/7Qrqel8iabg, transcript pasted by Pushpam 23 Nov 2026) used as research only. Updated with 2026 news: US-Israel war on Iran from 28 Feb 2026, Iranian missile/drone strikes on all six GCC states, Hormuz disruption, fresh Iranian attacks on Bahrain/Kuwait 17-18 Nov 2026 (Al Jazeera), Brent ~$92 mid-Nov 2026. Original script, en-GB.

## Cold open

1. **[WIDE]** This year, Iran fired thousands of missiles and drones at six small Gulf kingdoms. Airports, refineries, American bases. All of them were targeted.
   - Visual: Night Gulf city skyline; interceptor streaks arc up and burst; sirens; a family on a balcony looks up.
   - On screen: 2026
2. **[MED]** And every one of those six royal families is still in charge. Not a single crown dropped.
   - Visual: Six royal figures in a row on a palace terrace, unbothered; six gold crowns in the tracker set upright with a ding.
   - On screen: 6 / 6 STILL STANDING
3. **[MAP]** Which is odd, because the last century was a royal massacre round here. Egypt's king, gone. Iraq's, gone. Libya's, gone. The Shah of Iran, gone.
   - Visual: Map of the Middle East; crowns over Egypt, Iraq, Libya and Iran fall off one by one and crack; six Gulf crowns stay.
   - On screen: KINGS WHO FELL
4. **[MED]** And no, it isn't just oil. Iraq, Libya and Iran had oil too, and their kings fell anyway. These Gulf families nearly lost everything, more than once. They survived by rebuilding the game around themselves.
   - Visual: An oil barrel rolls in and gets crossed out; a sheikh rebuilds a toppling house of cards into a fort.
   - On screen: NOT JUST OIL
5. **[TITLE]** So how do the Gulf kings never fall?
   - Visual: Title over a single pearl rolling to a stop beside a gold crown.
   - On screen: WHY GULF KINGS NEVER FALL

## Act 1: Pearls and Protectors

1. **[WIDE]** Rewind to the early eighteen hundreds. The Gulf coast is hot, poor and small. A string of towns living off pearl diving and trade, each run by its own family.
   - Visual: Sun-baked coast with mud-brick houses and wooden dhows; a diver surfaces with a pearl.
   - On screen: EARLY 1800s
2. **[MED]** Many of those families are cousins. The Al Khalifa, who took Bahrain in 1783 and still rule it today, are related to the Al Sabah of Kuwait.
   - Visual: Two sheikhs in a fort courtyard embrace; a family-tree vine grows between their name tags.
   - On screen: AL KHALIFA · AL SABAH
3. **[MED]** Then the British arrive. But mostly, they don't conquer. Britain wants a quiet sea road to India, and the sheikhs want a powerful friend to keep rivals away. So deals are signed, one by one, from 1820 to Qatar in 1916.
   - Visual: A British naval officer and a sheikh sign under a tent on the beach; a ship offshore; treaty scrolls stack up with dates.
   - On screen: 1820 → 1916
4. **[MED]** In the late 1920s, Britain lets Bahrain's pearl captains bring in foreign crews, as long as each captain sends his men home after the season. Remember that little rule. It grows into something huge.
   - Visual: A pearl captain on a dhow ticks names on a list as foreign divers board; a tag 'SPONSOR' sticks to him.
   - On screen: THE CAPTAIN'S RULE
5. **[MED]** Then the pearl trade collapses, crushed by the Depression and cheap cultured pearls. But in 1932, oil turns up in Bahrain. And the money flows straight to the families Britain already protects.
   - Visual: Pearls in a market bowl turn grey; a derrick gushes black oil; coins pour into a sheikh's chest.
   - On screen: 1932 · OIL
6. **[MED]** So these weren't strong states. They were protected ones. Which raises a question. What happens when the bodyguard leaves?
   - Visual: A sheikh stands behind a big British officer like a shield; the officer looks at his watch.
   - On screen: WHAT IF HE LEAVES?

## Act 2: When Kings Fell

1. **[MED]** 1952. Army officers topple Egypt's king. Suddenly, monarchy looks like a leftover from the colonial age.
   - Visual: Officers in green march into a palace hall; a crown rolls off a throne.
   - On screen: EGYPT · 1952
2. **[WIDE]** Then July 1958, Iraq. Another British-backed kingdom. Brigadier Abdul Karim Qasim seizes power in a single day. The young King Faisal the Second is killed, and Iraq is a republic by nightfall.
   - Visual: Tanks in front of a Baghdad palace at dusk; a radio announcer; the flag on the palace changes.
   - On screen: IRAQ · 14 JULY 1958
3. **[MED]** In 1961, Kuwait's protection deal with Britain ends, and Qasim claims Kuwait belongs to Iraq. Britain rushes in troops, and Iraq backs off. Lesson learned. Small states survive because someone bigger stands behind them.
   - Visual: Desert border: Iraqi officer points at a map; British soldiers jog in; officer folds the map away.
   - On screen: KUWAIT · 1961
4. **[MAP]** North Yemen's Imam falls in 1962. And in 1965, a rebellion in Oman's Dhofar region becomes a Marxist insurgency, armed by China and the Soviet Union.
   - Visual: Map: Yemen crown falls; red sparks spread across southern Oman with small arrows from far away.
   - On screen: DHOFAR · 1965
5. **[MED]** Then, in January 1968, Prime Minister Harold Wilson drops a bombshell. Britain, short of money after devaluing the pound, will pull its forces out of the Gulf by the end of 1971.
   - Visual: A London office; a stylised prime minister at a desk with a pipe reads a statement; a falling pound coin.
   - On screen: BRITAIN IS LEAVING
6. **[MED]** A year later, Gaddafi topples Libya's king. The bodyguard is packing his bags, and revolution is next door.
   - Visual: British officer packs a suitcase in the fort courtyard while a sheikh watches nervously; the tracker crowns wobble.
   - On screen: THE BODYGUARD LEAVES

## Act 3: Remove the King, Save the Crown

1. **[MED]** Now for the strangest move in the whole story. Oman's Sultan Said bin Taimur is sitting on oil money, and spending almost none of it.
   - Visual: An old sultan in a dim palace room counts coins into a locked chest.
   - On screen: SULTAN SAID BIN TAIMUR
2. **[MED]** By 1970, roughly six hundred and seventy thousand Omanis have just two small hospitals and a handful of schools. And his own son, Qaboos, trained at Sandhurst, is kept under virtual house arrest.
   - Visual: A son in a uniform looks out of a barred window; counters show 2 HOSPITALS; a Sandhurst cap on the table.
   - On screen: 2 HOSPITALS
3. **[MED]** On the twenty-third of July 1970, Qaboos overthrows his father in a palace coup, with British officers helping. The old sultan is flown out on an RAF plane, and he never comes back.
   - Visual: Night palace courtyard; the son walks in with officers; an RAF transport plane takes off over the coast.
   - On screen: 23 JULY 1970
4. **[MED]** The family stays. Only the king changes. Two years later, Qatar does its own version. Sheikh Khalifa pushes the emir aside while he's abroad, without a shot fired.
   - Visual: A royal chair: one figure stands up and another sits; name plates swap.
   - On screen: SAME FAMILY · NEW RULER
5. **[MED]** Qaboos does what his father wouldn't. He spends the money on his people, brings in help from Britain, Iran and Jordan, and gives defeated rebels jobs in government. By the mid-seventies, the insurgency is beaten.
   - Visual: Dhofar mountains: a clinic and a school go up; a former rebel shakes hands and gets an ID badge.
   - On screen: REBELS → OFFICIALS
6. **[MED]** That's the secret rule of the Gulf. The family works like a company. Lose a boardroom fight, and you line up behind the winner. Because a civil war means everyone loses everything.
   - Visual: Royal family around a long table; one cousin loses a vote, sighs, and moves his chair behind the winner.
   - On screen: THE FAMILY FIRM

## Act 4: The Deal

1. **[WIDE]** Britain leaves in 1971. Bahrain and Qatar become independent, the emirates join together as the U A E, and then comes the jackpot. Between 1972 and 1982, oil goes from about three dollars a barrel to about thirty.
   - Visual: 1970s oil field at sunset; a price board flips from $3 to $30; tankers on the horizon.
   - On screen: $3 → $30
2. **[MED]** So the rulers offer their citizens a deal. No income tax. A well-paid government job, sooner or later. Free healthcare, free education, cheap power, even land.
   - Visual: A ministry counter: a citizen is handed a stack of gift boxes labelled JOB, HEALTH, SCHOOL, LAND.
   - On screen: THE DEAL
3. **[MED]** Today, around two in three working Saudis, and roughly nine in ten working Emiratis, are employed by the state.
   - Visual: Office of identical desks; bar chart 66% and 90% grows on the wall.
   - On screen: 66% · 90%
4. **[MED]** The price? No real say in who runs the country. No taxation, no representation. The old American slogan, flipped on its head.
   - Visual: A ballot box with a padlock; a sign 'NO TAXATION WITHOUT REPRESENTATION' flips upside down.
   - On screen: NO TAX · NO VOTE
5. **[WIDE]** But somebody has to build the towers and run the ports. So the rulers bring in millions of foreign workers. And after the boom, they switch from Arab workers to South Asians, partly out of fear that Arabs would bring revolutionary politics with them.
   - Visual: Construction site with cranes and a half-built tower; workers in hard hats carry beams; buses unload more.
   - On screen: MILLIONS OF WORKERS
6. **[MED]** Remember the pearl captain's rule? It grew into kafala, the sponsorship system that ties workers to their employers. Today, foreigners are about four in ten people in Oman, and close to nine in ten in Qatar and the U A E.
   - Visual: A worker's passport held by a boss; population bars for Oman 40%, Qatar 88%, UAE 88%.
   - On screen: KAFALA
7. **[MED]** So the people doing most of the work can't vote, can't organise, and can be sent home. And the citizens on top have far too much to lose to start a revolution.
   - Visual: A small group of citizens on a raised platform; a much bigger crowd of workers below, separated by a rope.
   - On screen: TOO MUCH TO LOSE

## Act 5: Threats from Every Side

1. **[MED]** But money doesn't buy everyone. In early 1979, the Shah of Iran, the mightiest king in the region, is overthrown.
   - Visual: Tehran street: huge crowd; a portrait frame falls; a crown on the map shatters.
   - On screen: IRAN · 1979
2. **[WIDE]** Then, on the twentieth of November 1979, during dawn prayers, armed men seize the Grand Mosque in Mecca and call for the fall of the House of Saud. The siege lasts two weeks, and hundreds die.
   - Visual: Generic grand mosque courtyard at dawn seen from far away, smoke rising; a news ticker; no gore.
   - On screen: MECCA · 20 NOV 1979
3. **[MED]** The Saudis crush the rebels. Then, quietly, they adopt much of their agenda. They slow down Westernisation and hand the clerics more power. Bend, don't break.
   - Visual: A king figure takes a poster of a modern woman off a wall and hands a big book to a cleric; a branch bends without snapping.
   - On screen: BEND, DON'T BREAK
4. **[MED]** With revolutionary Iran next door, the six kings form a club. In May 1981, they found the Gulf Cooperation Council. On paper, a defence pact. In practice, kings protecting kings.
   - Visual: Summit hall: six royals at a round table link hands; six flags without emblems; a door marked GCC.
   - On screen: GCC · 1981
5. **[WIDE]** Then, at about two in the morning on the second of August 1990, Saddam Hussein sends around a hundred thousand troops into Kuwait. The emir escapes, and Kuwait is declared Iraq's nineteenth province.
   - Visual: Night Kuwait City skyline; tanks rumble along a highway; a car speeds away towards Saudi Arabia.
   - On screen: KUWAIT · 2 AUG 1990
6. **[MED]** An American-led coalition throws Iraq out. The ground war lasts just a hundred hours, and the Al Sabah family is back on the throne.
   - Visual: Desert at dawn: jets fly over; the Kuwait crown in the tracker springs back up; a returning emir waves.
   - On screen: 100 HOURS
7. **[MED]** And here's the big shift. America takes over Britain's old job as bodyguard. Except this time, the kings pay for it. Qatar's Al Udeid becomes the biggest American base in the Middle East.
   - Visual: Air base: a sheikh hands a cheque to a US general beside a runway; cargo jets taxi.
   - On screen: THE NEW BODYGUARD

## Act 6: The Arab Spring

1. **[MED]** 2011. The Arab Spring. Within a year, the rulers of Tunisia, Egypt, Libya and Yemen are gone.
   - Visual: Map: four leader portraits topple like dominoes towards the Gulf, stopping at the edge.
   - On screen: 2011
2. **[MED]** The Gulf's first answer is cash. Saudi Arabia pledges about a hundred and thirty billion dollars for jobs, wages and housing. Oman creates tens of thousands of public jobs. And the rich neighbours pledge ten billion dollars each to Oman and Bahrain.
   - Visual: A royal pours money from a bucket into a pipe that runs to houses; counter $130B.
   - On screen: $130 BILLION
3. **[WIDE]** The second answer is force. In Bahrain, a Shia majority ruled by a Sunni royal family fills the Pearl Roundabout from the fourteenth of February. At first, they want a constitutional monarchy, not the end of the king.
   - Visual: A large roundabout square with a tall pearl monument; crowds with flags and signs 'NEW CONSTITUTION'.
   - On screen: BAHRAIN · 14 FEB 2011
4. **[MED]** On the fourteenth of March, about a thousand Saudi troops and five hundred Emirati police cross into Bahrain under the G C C banner. Martial law follows, and the Pearl monument is bulldozed.
   - Visual: Armoured vehicles cross a causeway; the pearl monument is gone, leaving an empty square with tyre marks.
   - On screen: 14 MARCH 2011
5. **[MED]** That's the whole system in one picture. Money where there's enough of it, neighbouring kings where there isn't, and outside protectors who look the other way.
   - Visual: Three-panel card: money bag, linked hands, a general with eyes covered.
   - On screen: MONEY · NEIGHBOURS · PROTECTORS

## Act 7: One Man, One Big Test

1. **[MED]** Then oil crashes, from over a hundred dollars in 2014 to under thirty by early 2016. Saudi Arabia cuts bonuses for civil servants, faces fury online, and quietly brings them back within months.
   - Visual: A price chart dives; an office worker reads his phone angrily; a bonus envelope flies back to him.
   - On screen: $100 → $30
2. **[MED]** Its bigger answer is one man. In 2017, Mohammed bin Salman becomes crown prince. That November, nearly four hundred princes and tycoons are held inside the Ritz-Carlton in Riyadh.
   - Visual: A luxury hotel lobby with chandeliers; men in robes sit on camp beds under the chandeliers; a guard at the door.
   - On screen: RIYADH · NOV 2017
3. **[MED]** For decades, the Saudi royals ruled by consensus among senior princes. Now power runs through one person. Stronger, yes. But everything rests on one pair of shoulders.
   - Visual: A long table of princes fades to one chair; a giant crown balances on one figure's shoulders.
   - On screen: ONE PAIR OF SHOULDERS
4. **[MED]** Kuwait is heading the same way. In May 2024, the emir shut down the Gulf's liveliest elected parliament and suspended parts of the constitution. Nobody took to the streets.
   - Visual: An empty parliament chamber; a door closes and a padlock clicks; an empty street outside.
   - On screen: KUWAIT · MAY 2024
5. **[WIDE]** And then came 2026. War between America and Iran, and thousands of Iranian missiles and drones fired at Gulf airports, refineries and American bases. Even this month, Bahrain and Kuwait were under attack again. Still, no palace fell.
   - Visual: Night skyline with interceptor streaks again (callback to the cold open); the six crowns in the tracker wobble and hold.
   - On screen: 2026 · STILL STANDING
6. **[MED]** But the real danger isn't a missile. In 2020, the I M F warned the Gulf's roughly two trillion dollars of financial wealth could be used up by 2034. And the world has agreed to move away from the very fuel that pays for the deal.
   - Visual: A sovereign wealth vault; gold stacks shrink; an hourglass with oil instead of sand runs low.
   - On screen: $2 TRILLION · 2034?
7. **[MED]** These kings survived by swapping protectors, swapping workers, and even swapping kings. The question for the next hundred years is what they swap when the oil money runs dry.
   - Visual: The pearl from act 1 sits beside an almost empty oil barrel; the royal looks from one to the other.
   - On screen: WHAT DO THEY SWAP NEXT?
