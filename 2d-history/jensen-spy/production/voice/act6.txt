Beijing, 1983. China founds the Ministry of State Security. It does the jobs America splits between the CIA and the FBI: spying abroad, and catching spies at home.

Much of its work abroad goes through people with real lives and real jobs. Find someone close to what you want, and become their friend. Ask for something small, and then for more.

In 2017 a ministry officer invites an engineer from GE Aviation, which makes jet engines, to give a talk in China. Dinner, then questions about the engines. But the engineer has gone to the FBI, and the officer ends up with twenty years in an American prison.

And sometimes it's romance. In 1982 the FBI turns a Los Angeles businesswoman, Katrina Leung, into a source. Code name: Parlor Maid. For about twenty years, prosecutors will say, she's sleeping with her handler.

In 2003 the FBI accuses her of copying a secret document her handler took home, and arrests them both. The case falls apart in court. She pleads guilty only to lying to the FBI and to a tax charge.

In 2011 a Chinese student named Christine Fang shows up in Bay Area politics. She helps raise money for a young congressman, Eric Swalwell. In 2015 the FBI gives him a defensive briefing, a warning, and he cuts her off. She leaves the country and is never charged.
