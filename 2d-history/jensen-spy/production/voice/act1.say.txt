This is Alice Hwahng. Her Chinese name is Hwahng Luh. She's a Chinese citizen, and despite the name, she's no relation of his.

According to the Times, she starts out as a reporter for Chinese state television. Then a private banker at big Chinese banks. Then an executive at a Shanghai fund.

In twenty twenty-three she takes over a small Singapore company, and it gets a new name: Mega-speed. It began as the Singapore arm of a Chinese gaming company with state-backed investors.

Mega-speed buys En-vidia's best chips, puts them in data centres in Malaysia and Indonesia, and rents out the computing power. One customer, the Financial Times reports, is Ali-baba. That way Ali-baba uses chips it can't buy in China.

According to the Times, about two billion dollars of En-vidia's most advanced chips go through Mega-speed's Malaysian arm. Bloomberg calls it the single largest buyer of En-vidia chips in Southeast Asia.

When the Times visits its Singapore office on a weekday, there are two employees. When American inspectors visit its data centre in Malaysia, they find equipment still in its original boxes. Where its money comes from is unknown.
