On Sunday, Nvidia posts an email from Jensen to everyone who works there. 'I owe my family and all of you an apology. I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did.'

Ten days earlier, he'd called it all completely normal. He doesn't name the woman. According to the Times, he's told his board who it was.

The board opens an investigation, run by its independent directors with outside lawyers. It says it has no reason to believe any secrets were lost, and it backs him to stay.

That night, a reporter asks Trump whether he wishes he'd acted on the intelligence. 'No,' he says, 'I don't think so.' He calls Jensen an honourable guy and says he should stay on as CEO.

Alice Huang hasn't said a word. She's never been charged with anything. Megaspeed didn't answer the Financial Times.

On the seventeenth, Nvidia reports its earnings. On the eighteenth, Congress wants him in that closed room in Washington. The same day, China hosts the Asia-Pacific summit in Shenzhen, and Trump is going.
