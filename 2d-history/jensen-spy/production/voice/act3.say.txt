Kentucky, nineteen seventy-three. A nine-year-old boy from Taiwan arrives at a boarding school in the hills, with his older brother. His parents have sold nearly everything to pay for it.

But it isn't the school they think it is. It's a reform school for troubled kids. His job there is cleaning the toilets, every day.

His roommate is seventeen, covered in tattoos and knife scars. Jensen teaches him to read. In return, the roommate teaches him to bench press.

In Oregon, from fifteen, he works nights at a Denny's: dishwasher, then busboy, then waiter. At Oregon State, his lab partner is a student named Lori Mills. They marry in nineteen eighty-five.

In nineteen ninety-three, in a booth at a Denny's in San Jose, he and two engineers start a company to make graphics chips. They put in two hundred dollars each. They call it En-vidia.

The first chip flops. By nineteen ninety-seven they have about a month of payroll left.
