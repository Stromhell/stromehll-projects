Beijing, nineteen eighty-three. China founds the Ministry of State Security. It does the jobs America splits between the C I A and the F B I: spying abroad, and catching spies at home.

Much of its work abroad goes through people with real lives and real jobs. Find someone close to what you want, and become their friend. Ask for something small, and then for more.

In twenty seventeen a ministry officer invites an engineer from G E Aviation, which makes jet engines, to give a talk in China. Dinner, then questions about the engines. But the engineer has gone to the F B I, and the officer ends up with twenty years in an American prison.

And sometimes it's romance. In nineteen eighty-two the F B I turns a Los Angeles businesswoman, Katrina Lee-ung, into a source. Code name: Parlor Maid. For about twenty years, prosecutors will say, she's sleeping with her handler.

In two thousand three the F B I accuses her of copying a secret document her handler took home, and arrests them both. The case falls apart in court. She pleads guilty only to lying to the F B I and to a tax charge.

In twenty eleven a Chinese student named Christine Fang shows up in Bay Area politics. She helps raise money for a young congressman, Eric Swall-well. In twenty fifteen the F B I gives him a defensive briefing, a warning, and he cuts her off. She leaves the country and is never charged.
