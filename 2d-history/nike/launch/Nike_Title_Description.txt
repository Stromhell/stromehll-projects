Description:
In 1971 a track coach poured rubber into his wife's waffle iron and ruined it. That waffle iron helped start a company that now sells around 46 billion dollars a year, and it owns almost none of the factories that make its shoes.

Bill Bowerman hated heavy shoes. His runner Phil Knight flew to Japan in 1962, invented a company name on the spot, and sold Onitsuka Tigers from the trunk of his car. Then came a 35-dollar logo, a name that came to Jeff Johnson in a dream, air in the sole, Michael Jordan (who wanted Adidas), Just Do It, the sweatshop scandal, Vaporfly, and the stumble that brought a former intern back as CEO.
