Oregon, the nineteen fifties. Bill Bowerman coached track at the University of Oregon, and he had a thing about heavy shoes.

His maths: shave one ounce off a shoe and, over a mile of strides, a runner lifts hundreds of pounds less.

So he pulled his runners' shoes apart in his workshop and rebuilt them by hand. His athletes were his test subjects, whether they liked it or not.

One of those runners was a middle-distance man called Phil Knight. Good, not great. But he listened.

At Stanford business school in nineteen sixty-two, Knight wrote a paper: Japanese running shoes could do to German brands like Adidas what Japanese cameras had done to German cameras.

That same year, aged 24, he flew to Japan and walked into the offices of Onitsuka in Kobe, makers of the Tiger running shoe.

They asked which company he represented. He didn't have one. So he made one up on the spot: Blue Ribbon Sports.

It worked. Onitsuka let him sell Tigers in the western United States.
