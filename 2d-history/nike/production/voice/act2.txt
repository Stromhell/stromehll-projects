In 1964, Knight and Bowerman put in 500 dollars each. Blue Ribbon Sports was real.

Knight sold the shoes from the trunk of his car at track meets across the Pacific Northwest.

First year: about 1,300 pairs, roughly 8,000 dollars. He kept his day job as an accountant.

His first full-time employee, Jeff Johnson, wrote personal letters to customers, noted their race times and even their birthdays, and opened the first store in Santa Monica.

Meanwhile Bowerman wrote a little book called Jogging, which helped set off the American running boom. The coach wasn't just making shoes. He was making customers.

Then, in 1971, came the waffle iron. Ruined, as we saw. But its grid of squares became the waffle sole: grip without weight.

The Bowermans had to buy a new waffle iron.
