Nike's first star was Steve Prefontaine, a fearless front-runner from Oregon. In 1975 he died in a car crash, aged 24. Nike's headquarters still honours him.

Then an aerospace engineer, Frank Rudy, pitched an odd idea that other companies had turned down: pockets of pressurised air inside the sole.

Nike said yes. In 1978 it launched its first air-cushioned shoe. Nike Air would become one of the most valuable names in footwear.

In 1980 the company went public. The accountant with a trunk full of shoes was suddenly very rich.

In 1987, designer Tinker Hatfield, who had trained as an architect, cut a window in the sole so you could see the air. The Air Max.

Here's the real trick. Nike designs shoes, then pays contract factories, mostly in Asia, to make them. Today about half its shoes come from Vietnam.

It spends its energy on what happens in your head when you see the swoosh. Anyone can copy a shoe. It's much harder to copy a feeling.
