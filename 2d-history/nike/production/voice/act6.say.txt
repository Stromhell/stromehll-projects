Then the same machine that made Nike cool turned the world against it. Reporters found low wages, punishing hours and, in some supplier factories, children.

In nineteen ninety-six, a magazine printed a photo of a boy in Pakistan stitching footballs with the swoosh. Campus protests followed across America.

In nineteen ninety-eight, Knight admitted the Nike name had become linked with slave wages, forced overtime and abuse. Nike raised minimum ages, allowed independent audits and later published its supplier list.

Critics said it wasn't enough. Supporters said Nike changed faster than most of the industry. That argument still goes on.
