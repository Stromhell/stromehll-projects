By the early 1970s, Blue Ribbon and Onitsuka didn't trust each other. Each suspected the other was about to cut it out.

Knight needed his own brand, fast. For a logo, he asked Carolyn Davidson, a design student he'd met at Portland State, for something that suggested motion.

At two dollars an hour, it came to 35 dollars. Knight's verdict: he didn't love it, but maybe it would grow on him.

The name came even closer to the deadline. Knight wanted 'Dimension Six'. Nobody else did.

Then Jeff Johnson said a word had come to him in a dream: Nike, the Greek goddess of victory. The boxes had to be printed, so Nike it was.

Davidson didn't stay a 35-dollar footnote. In 1983, Knight gave her a gold swoosh ring with a diamond, and Nike shares.

In 1972, the first Nike-branded shoes showed up at the US Olympic trials in Eugene, including a hand-made racer runners called the Moon Shoe.

Money was so tight that American banks kept refusing credit. A Japanese trading company, Nissho Iwai, kept the business afloat.

The Onitsuka split went to court. In 1974, Blue Ribbon Sports won.
