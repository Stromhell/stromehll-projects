In nineteen seventy-one, a track coach poured rubber into his wife's waffle iron. It glued shut and never made a waffle again.

That ruined waffle iron helped start a company that now sells around 46 billion dollars of shoes and clothes a year.

And here's the strange part: Nike owns almost none of the factories that make its shoes.

This is the story of a made-up company name, a 35-dollar logo, a basketball player who wanted to sign with someone else, and a slogan with a very dark origin.
