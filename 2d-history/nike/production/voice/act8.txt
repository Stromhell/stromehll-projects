In 2020, former eBay boss John Donahoe took over. His bet: sell directly through Nike's own apps and stores, and drop many of the shops that had sold Nike for decades.

For a while it worked. Then shoppers found other brands on the shelves Nike had left, and Nike leaned on re-releasing old favourites.

Remember the waffle iron? The company that once ruined kitchen appliances to invent something new was mostly selling its greatest hits.

Newer running brands like On and Hoka won over runners Nike used to own, and Nike's share price fell by more than half from its peak.

In October 2024, Nike brought back Elliott Hill as CEO. He'd joined as an intern in 1988 and spent over 30 years there.

His plan: focus on athletes again, win back runners, footballers and basketball players, and make peace with the shops Nike had walked away from.

The numbers are still mixed. Revenue has been roughly flat at around 46 billion dollars, with sales in China and Converse falling hard, while running, football and basketball are growing again.

And remember Onitsuka? It became part of ASICS, which has spent recent years winning back serious runners. The company that gave Knight his first shoes is now one of his rivals.
