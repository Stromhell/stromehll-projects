Imagine ee-Bay and TikTok had a baby. You scroll through endless live streams, and in each one somebody is selling something in real time. Trainers, coins, toys, designer bags. And, above all, trading cards.

Anyone can sell. You open a shop, go live, and Whatnot takes a cut of up to eight per cent. It runs in America, Canada, Australia, several EU countries, and here in the UK.

But the real action is something called a box break. A seller opens a sealed box of cards live on camera, and viewers buy a spot: a share of whatever comes out.

In a randomised break, you don't choose your team. You pay first, and then a randomiser hands you one. Good team, great cards. Bad team, bad luck. Sometimes your team isn't even in the box.

Mystery repacks work the same way. You buy a pack without knowing what's inside, or what it's worth. The host rips it open, the chat goes wild, and somewhere, someone wins big.

Whatnot says every buyer must get at least one card per break, and anyone sent an empty envelope gets a refund. But remember one word: chance. This whole story hangs on it.
