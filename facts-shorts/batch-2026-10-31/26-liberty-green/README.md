## Idea
The copper Statue of Liberty was reddish-brown in 1886 and turned green over about 30 years as a protective patina formed.

## Voiceover (64 words)
The Statue of Liberty wasn't always green. When it was finished in eighteen eighty-six, it was a shiny reddish-brown, like a brand new penny, because its skin is made of copper. Over about thirty years, air and rain reacted with the copper and slowly built up a green coat called patina. That green layer actually protects the metal underneath, so nobody cleans it off.
