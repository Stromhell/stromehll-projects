## Idea
Stars are point sources, so air turbulence bends their thin beam; planets are tiny discs whose wobbles average out.

## Voiceover (69 words)
Why do stars twinkle, but planets don't? Stars are so far away that their light reaches us as a single tiny point. As it passes through our moving, wobbly air, that thin beam gets bent this way and that, so the star flickers. Planets are much closer, so they look like tiny discs. Their wobbles average out, and they shine steady. Up in space, stars don't twinkle at all.
