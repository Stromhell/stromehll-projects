## Idea
The Moon spins once per orbit (tidal locking), so the same side always faces Earth.

## Voiceover (68 words)
Why do we only ever see one side of the Moon? It does spin, but it spins exactly once for every trip around Earth. Long ago, Earth's gravity stretched the Moon into a slight egg shape and kept tugging on that bulge, slowing its spin until the two matched. Scientists call it tidal locking. So the same face always points at us, and the far side stays hidden.
