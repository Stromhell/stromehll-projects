- YouTube title: Why plane windows are NEVER square
- Caption:

```
The first jet airliner had square windows. It did not end well.
In 1954 two de Havilland Comets broke apart mid-air. Tests in a giant water tank showed cracks growing from the sharp window corners, where stress piles up. Rounded corners spread that stress out evenly.

- Caption:

```
Astronauts float with no weight. So why do they come home weaker?
Your bones rebuild themselves based on the load they carry. In space your legs and hips carry almost nothing, so the body takes bone away, around 1 to 2 percent a month. That's why space station crews exercise every single day.

100 minutes in an hour would be easier. So why 60?
Blame the Babylonians. They counted in sixties, and 60 splits evenly into halves, thirds, quarters, fifths, sixths and tenths. Their astronomers used it to track the sky and time. We still use it for hours, minutes, seconds and the 360 degrees of a circle.

A web traps flies. So why doesn't the spider get stuck?
In 2012 Smithsonian researchers looked closely at spider legs. Branching hairs mean only a few tips touch the glue, and the legs carry a non-stick coating. Wash it off and the legs stick much more. Spiders also move carefully.

```
Almost everything sinks when it freezes solid. Not water.
When water freezes, its molecules lock into a roomy crystal, so ice is lighter than the same amount of water. Lakes freeze from the top down, and the ice works like a lid that keeps the water below liquid all winter. Lucky fish.

## 11-leap-year
- Video: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1H9nmlVziggVZNFwY48aBm-foJGns1f50/view
- Cover: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1mm_ZqtIpowlYASHIU3Dv_geFvTLf2JH_/view
- YouTube title: Why February 29 exists (and why 1900 skipped it)
- Caption:

```
Why does February get an extra day every four years?
Earth takes about 365 and a quarter days to orbit the Sun. Skip that quarter and the calendar slips almost a day every four years, more than three weeks a century. Adding February 29 overshoots a little, so most century years skip it.
Sources: Royal Museums Greenwich: Which years are leap years?; Astronomy.com: Why do we have leap days.
