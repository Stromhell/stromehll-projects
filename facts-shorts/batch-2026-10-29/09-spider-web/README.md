## Idea
Spiders avoid sticking to their own webs thanks to branching leg hairs, a non-stick coating and careful movement.

## Voiceover (68 words)
A spider web is sticky enough to trap flies. So why doesn't the spider get stuck? In 2012, Smithsonian researchers looked closely at spider legs. They're covered in branching hairs, so only a few tips touch the glue. The legs also carry a non-stick coating. When the scientists washed it off, the legs stuck much more. And spiders move carefully, so the glue never gets a good grip.
