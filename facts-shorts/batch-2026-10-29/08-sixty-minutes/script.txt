Why does an hour have sixty minutes and not a hundred? Blame the Babylonians. Thousands of years ago, they counted in sixties. Sixty splits evenly into halves, thirds, quarters, fifths, sixths, tenths and more. That made fractions easy without decimals. Their astronomers used it to track the sky and time. We still use it for hours, minutes, seconds, and the three hundred and sixty degrees of a circle.
