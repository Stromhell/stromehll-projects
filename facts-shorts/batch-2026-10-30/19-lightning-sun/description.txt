One bolt of lightning is hotter than the surface of the Sun.
The Sun's surface is about 5,500°C. Lightning heats the air around it to about 30,000°C in a split second. That air blasts outward so fast it makes a shock wave, and that boom is thunder.
Sources: US National Weather Service; US National Weather Service.
