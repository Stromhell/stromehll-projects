## Why each line
- **Rothschilds = 2D.** A money-and-power story told through maps, loans, family trees and five brothers in five cities. It sits with BlackRock and JPMorgan as a "money dynasties" run, and 2D cutouts handle bonds, charts and cities fastest.
- **Doolittle Raid = 3D.** Sixteen bombers launching off a carrier deck in rough seas, low-level runs over Tokyo, crash landings in China at night. It is one continuous action story, already written with dialogue, which is exactly what the 3D engine is for. Aircraft and ocean sets can reuse the Thunderbolt and Bermuda builds.
- **Nike = 2D.** A founder story with logos, a waffle iron, ads and sales charts. Fast cutout style, same treatment as NVIDIA.
- **Suez Canal = 2D dynamic.** Pharaohs, Darius, Rome, Napoleon, de Lesseps, cholera camps, steam dredgers: 3,000 years of painted desert scenes. The Village Painterly look sells period settings like this, and it leads straight into the Rothschild video (Britain bought the canal shares in 1875 with a Rothschild loan).
- **Napoleon = 2D dynamic.** A character drama (rise, Russia, exile, Waterloo, Saint Helena) in period costume across Europe. Village Painterly suits it, and it fits between Spain part 1 and part 2 in era.
- **World War 2, Part 1 = 2D.** Versailles to the fall of France in 1940. It is maps, arrows and timelines (Austria, Sudetenland, Poland, Blitzkrieg, Dunkirk). 2D maps do it best.
- **Micromax = 2D.** An Indian business rise-and-fall story (PCO phones, the 30-day battery phone, Jio, Chinese brands). Strong for the Indian audience; cutout style like NVIDIA.
- **Changez Khan in Islam = skip.** It is told as a religious prophecy (a hadith, Gog and Magog), with a present-day Palestine comparison and one-sided lines about Christians. That is risky and off-brand for a neutral history channel, and the Mongol Empire video is already in 2D production. Its best story beats (the Khwarazm envoy killed, the fall of Baghdad in 1258, Ain Jalut in 1260) can go into the Mongol video or a reel.

## Going viral

**1. The Rothschilds (2D)**
- Hook: "In 1875 Britain needed 4 million pounds by the next morning to buy the Suez Canal. The government didn't have it. One family did."
- Title: "How One Family Became Richer Than Kings".
- Thumbnail: five brothers' portraits over a map of Europe with arrows to London, Paris, Vienna, Naples and Frankfurt. Text: "5 BROTHERS. 5 CAPITALS."
- Reels: the overnight Suez loan; Nathan's courier and the Waterloo news; the five arrows on the family crest; lending to both Britain and Napoleon; Credit-Anstalt's 1931 crash.
- Script must-dos: cut the 8,400 words to about 3,500. Treat the "Nathan made a fortune off Waterloo" story as a legend, and say so. No conspiracy framing at all: the Rothschild name is a magnet for antisemitic myths, so stay with documented loans and dates and include the family's persecution under the Nazis. Spelling fixes: Mayer Amschel (not "Meer / Maya Amshel"), Hesse-Kassel (not "Hessa Castle"), Simon Wolf Oppenheimer, Leopold I, Lehman Brothers, Creditanstalt.
