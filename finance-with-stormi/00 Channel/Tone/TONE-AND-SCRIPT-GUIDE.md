# Finance with Stormi: tone and script guide (v1, 12 Nov 2026)

Source: Pushpam's breakdown of the reference channel Paper Billions (@PaperBillionsTV, "Money stories, drawn on paper"), shared 12 Nov 2026 17:17 UTC. Pushpam: "this will be ur tonality; animation style will be from that thread" (rubber-hose style thread, see ../Style/README.md).
We copy the formula, never their characters, art, titles or wording.

## Reference numbers (as given by Pushpam, not re-checked)
3 uploads in 5 days, ~9.18K subs, ~2.62M views. Nvidia spy story (19:48) ~1.43M; market crash (15:00) ~1.10M; IPO accounting (14:09) ~95K. Story-driven thriller beat accounting breakdown by ~15x.

## Why it works (what we copy)
1. Newsjacking at documentary length: 15-20 min video within 48-72 h of a big NYT/Reuters/SEC story.
2. Packaging: huge known name + contradiction or thriller premise. 6-8 word titles, 5th-grade reading level, open curiosity gap.
3. Watch time: 14-20 min, pattern interrupt about every 30 s.
4. "Your wallet" pivot: every story is tied back to the viewer's money (401(k), index fund share, power bill).

## Script formula
**Cold open (first 45 s, 4 beats)**
1. 0:00-0:12 paradox scene or shock number (make it human scale: "$18 million an hour, nights included").
2. 0:12-0:25 the twist + a first 3-line micro-skit.
3. 0:25-0:38 the central question, in plain English ("This is the story of how...").
4. 0:38-0:45 rewind to a concrete date + city to start Act I.

**Body: narrator-to-skit loop.** Narrator never talks more than 35-45 s straight. Every legal, technical or accounting idea becomes a short skit between two characters. Unlike the reference, vary the skit rhythm (not always question -> one-word answer -> dry punchline).

**Zero-jargon rule.** Every big number or term gets a kitchen-table analogy (chef vs 10,000 line cooks; "rent for a brain"; dollars per American; "price of a used couch").

**Structure.** 6-7 named chapters, or numbered "cracks", or minute-by-minute timelines. End circular: return to the opening scene or line, now with new meaning.

## Visual + sound grammar (the look itself comes from the style thread)
- Moody, dark interiors lit by warm local light (lamps, candles, screens), heavy vignette, eye forced to centre.
- Data lives inside the scene: whiteboards, scales, cash stacks, rubber stamps (APPROVED / BLOCKED). No plain charts.
- 2.5D/3D perspective sets with 2D characters (matches the rubber-hose hybrid work).
- Narrator (warm, steady documentary male) + separate character voices for skits.
- Dense foley: marker squeaks, stamp thuds, paper, phones, shutters, rain, glasses.

## Where we beat them
1. Few black-screen text cards (they use 25+ per video). Use camera moves, match cuts and document zooms instead; keep cards for chapter titles.
2. Better acting: articulated rubber-hose rigs, 3/4 and profile turns, real poses for serious moments.
3. More sets, less reuse of the same 3 rooms.
4. Prefer corporate/geopolitical THRILLER framing (hero, villain, cat and mouse) over one-day market recaps, so videos stay evergreen.
