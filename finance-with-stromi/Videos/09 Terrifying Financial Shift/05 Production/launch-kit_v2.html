Description 

On 5 August 2024, Japan's stock market fell 12.4% in a single day. No war, no earthquake, no pandemic. The trigger was a quarter-point rate rise in Tokyo and a money trick almost nobody outside Wall Street had heard of: the yen carry trade. In 2026 that rubber band is stretched again.

In this animated explainer, Nora, Chet, Hana, Kenji and the rest of the cast show how traders borrowed nearly free yen to buy U.S. bonds and big tech, how leverage turns a 5% move into 50%, why the band snapped in 2024, why record yen borrowing, Japan selling Treasuries and joint U.S.-Japan intervention in 2026 matter, what $40 trillion of U.S. debt and 5% Treasury yields mean for your mortgage, why central banks keep buying gold, and five calm things to check in your own wallet.
