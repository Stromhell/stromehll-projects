Every week, the US government holds an auction and asks the world one question: who will lend us money? For decades the answer was easy. Now the easiest lenders are walking out. So what actually happens if everyone stops buying US debt, and why does it end up in your mortgage, your groceries and your savings?

In this animated explainer, Rosa and Teo lose their 16th house offer and follow the trail back to the IOU Auction, where Tab (the US government), Madame Reserve (foreign central banks), Ferdie (the Fed), Flash (hedge funds) and Del (the dealer banks) bid on America's IOUs. We walk through what a Treasury bond really is, why foreign central banks are buying less (China's holdings are at their lowest since 2008), the October 2026 10-year auction that sold at 5.30%, the highest since 2000, the trillion-dollar interest bill, the Moody's downgrade, what it does to a $400k mortgage, why old bonds lose value when rates rise, March 2020 and SVB, and what is actually likely to happen: not doomsday, but a slow grind of higher rates.
